Emending Nonlinear Recurrence
Erik Garrison erik.garrison@gmail.com
Poietic PBC / UTHSC, Memphis, TN, USA
Abstract
Nonlinear-state recurrence grows executed compositional depth in time: E97 applies one
tanh-of-aﬃne state update per token. In the dual regime, a linear-state GDN fold with a
state-independent parameter schedule remains aﬃne in its initial state, with zero nonlinear
temporal depth. Lean 4 artifacts establish this structural duality, matched-signature delta-
versus-raw-write separation, and an S5 routing bridge; they do not lower-bound minimal
circuit depth or prove a full-stack impossibility. Width-axis multi-programming makes the
nonlinear route practical: a 1.3 B Emender trains through 150.8 B Pile tokens on one eight-
GPU workstation, while a parameter-matched, CMA-selected GDN2-MLP control occupies
the same training-loss band. Multi-seed small-scale iterated-map probes show a depth-wall
signature that is replicated on this trained 1.3 B pair: modular-quadratic terminal accuracy
is 99.96% versus 76.09% at trained length, and 76.07% versus 53.36% at eight times that
length. This is a single-seed, common-cap comparison with unequal realized training, not
invariant extrapolation. The negative transfer is explicit: “The 8M S5 separation does
NOT replicate at 1.3B on this pair.” Repeated nonlinear maps in feedback, optimization,
reservoirs, and dynamics deﬁne the applications scope, not demonstrated deployments. For
TMLR, the contribution is correctness-ﬁrst: inspectable Lean statements, artifact-derived
results, disclosed negatives, and numbered gaps, including recurrent tracker realization and
constrained aﬃne-channel realizability.

1 Introduction
A recurrent sequence model reads its input one step at a time, folding each token into an internal state that
summarizes everything seen so far. How such a model can be trained, and how fast, turns almost entirely
on a single design choice: how that state is updated from one step to the next. Over the past several years
the ﬁeld has converged on updates that are linear in the state. A linear update can be re-expressed as a
parallel scan over the whole sequence at once instead of a step-by-step loop, and that re-expression is what
lets recurrent models reach the training throughput of attention on modern hardware. State-space models
and gated linear-attention architectures — Mamba and its successors (Gu & Dao, 2024) (Dao & Gu, 2024)
(Lahoti et al., 2026), gated linear attention (Yang et al., 2023), RWKV (Peng et al., 2025), and the delta
rule and its gated form (Yang et al., 2024b) (Yang et al., 2024a) — now stand alongside the transformer as
the eﬃcient backbone of choice for long sequences.
This eﬃciency is bought with a structural concession. A state update that is linear in the state can be
parallelized, but it also bounds what the state can compute as the sequence grows: restricted linear-state
recurrence can have limited circuit complexity under explicit arithmetic and architectural assumptions; the
preﬁx products of a non-solvable group are a canonical diagnostic (Merrill et al., 2024) (Barrington, 1986).
A direct way to add nonlinear temporal computation is to pass the state through a nonlinearity at every
step. But a nonlinear-in-time update breaks the parallel scan and forces the time loop to run serially, and
serial training at billion-parameter scale was presumed too slow to be practical. The ﬁeld’s response has
been to route around the obstruction rather than meet it: parallelize the time axis by other means, such as
Newton iteration on a block-bidiagonal Jacobian (Danieli et al., 2025), or interleave recurrent layers with

attention so the hardest state-tracking work falls to the attention (Mishra et al., 2026) (Merrill et al., 2026)
(Behrouz et al., 2025) (De et al., 2024).
Two questions are left open by this detour. First, whether a purely nonlinear-in-time recurrence — no
parallel scan, no attention to lean on — can be trained to competitive scale at all, on a realistic training
budget. Second, once it can, whether the particular form of the nonlinear update rule makes any diﬀerence
to what the trained model can do. Neither had been tested directly, because the pure setting had been set
aside as the expensive case to avoid rather than the case to measure.
Here we show that it can be trained, and that the form of the update does matter. A purely nonlinear
recurrent language model — E97, the current 1.3 B-class instance of the Emender family — made trainable
by running many small recurrent programs in parallel across the network’s width rather than across time,
trains through 150.8 billion tokens on The Pile (Gao et al., 2020) using a single eight-GPU workstation,
with no cluster and no sequence parallelism, reaching an estimated 0.93 bits per byte (Section 5). Its linear-
recurrent baseline — GDN2-MLP, a Gated DeltaNet-2 (Hatamizadeh et al., 2026) mixer followed by the
same SwiGLU MLP, selected by the same CMA-ES (Hansen et al., 2003) search protocol at a parameter
count within 0.01% — trains on literally matched tokens under the same 2k-chunk Pile recipe and lands in
the same loss-versus-tokens band: at the matched 150.79-billion-token cutoﬀ the smoothed loss gap between
the two arms is 0.010 nats/token, with the sign of the gap depending on the averaging window (Section 5).
The 1.3 B result is therefore a replication across the update rule and tuning regime, not across scale: both
arms sit in the same 1.3 B-class parameter band, and whether the separation or the loss parity survives at 7 B
and beyond is named openly as a limitation in Section 10. On bulk training-log loss the two update families
are operationally tied, so language-modeling loss does not separate them. Their diﬀerences emerge instead
in state tracking. At 8 M scale, the delta-correcting update learns the S5 preﬁx-product task more eﬃciently
than the raw-write and GDN baselines under the tested protocols. At 1.3 B, the modular-quadratic probe
reproduces a depth-wall signature, but the S5 ordering does not transfer (Section 6). The Lean core proves
matched-signature one-step separation, exact non-equality at every ﬁnite k on a constructed witness, and a
120-state recognizer ﬂoor. Its generator-table lookup construction is not a proof that the tanh recurrence
implements the full tracker; this missing trajectory bridge is Open item F1 (Section 8). Neither the duality
nor the cited complexity results establish a blanket linear-state S5 impossibility.
Throughput on the width axis comes from many small recurrent programs running side by side, each stepping
its time loop serially, which is what makes the serial recurrence practical at scale.
The architecture is a residual stack of recurrent layers. Each layer pairs a matrix-state memory with a write
rule that has two halves. Delta correction is the ﬁrst half. The layer reads what the memory predicts at
the addressed slot, computes the prediction error, and writes the correction. Memory overwrites a stale
binding when a later token addresses the same slot with a diﬀerent value. Tanh-with-latching is the second
half. Under the hypotheses of theorem set F (Section 8), a saturated slot resists perturbations, keeps its
sign below a write threshold, and releases under a suﬃcient counter-delta. These are conditional slot-level
guarantees; the hold theorem EmenderLatching.emender_latch_holds_by_default assumes λ ≥ 1, not
the production decay below one. Persistent, revisable memory is the design motivation, not a proved default
behavior of the trained stack. An emender is one such layer; the Emender is the architecture family obtained
by stacking emender layers; E97 — the saturated delta-correcting cell extended with split erase/read and
value-write gates — is the 1.3 B instance evaluated throughout this paper, and E88 is the developmental
predecessor instance that preceded it (Section A).
A Lean 4 trusted core is the spine of the argument. The state-channel duality proves that E97 appends one
nonlinear update per step, while a GDN fold with a state-independent schedule stays aﬃne in its initial state.
It also proves matched-signature delta-versus-raw-write separation, split-gated relations, slot-wise latching
guarantees under stated hypotheses, and the S5 recognizer ﬂoor (Section 8). These are structural facts,
not application-level approximation lower bounds. The empirical work asks what SGD ﬁnds: at parameter-
matched 8 M scale the Emender reaches 0.79 on S5 against 0.36 for GDN and 0.22 for raw-write (Section 6),
whereas the current 1.3 B pair supplies a positive iterated-map result and a negative group-state result.
Width-axis multi-programming makes this theory-to-learning comparison practical.

Delta correction is one response; hybrids are another
The recurrent state-tracking problem admits more than one architectural response. Hybrid architectures
interleave recurrent layers with attention layers and recover much of what attention-free recurrence loses on
state tracking and long-range mixing. M2
RNN (Mishra et al., 2026), OLMo-Hybrid (Merrill et al., 2026),
Titans (Behrouz et al., 2025), and Griﬃn (De et al., 2024) are recent instances, and the limit-of-pure-
recurrence story is documented by Merrill, Petty and Sabharwal (Merrill et al., 2024). Delta correction is a
diﬀerent response. It does not add cross-token mixing; it changes what the recurrent state writes at each step
from a raw additive overwrite to a correction against the existing slot. The two responses are complementary,
not rival: a hybrid stack can use any update rule in its recurrent layers, and delta correction is well-deﬁned
wherever a matrix-state recurrent layer is.
We evaluate delta correction in the attention-free, time-serial recurrent arena, the pure nonlinear recurrent
(PNR) setting in the sense of "no cross-token attention mechanism"; gating, projections, normalizations and
other intra-token nonlinear operations are permitted and load-bearing (Section 3 ingredient (d) and Section 5
on gradient conditioning). The PNR arena is where the contrast is cleanest (a delta-vs-raw-write contrast
in the speciﬁed update-resource class, Section 8). Set D uses synthetic state-pass charges, excluding actual
dense HW and feature-network costs; it is not an actual matched-compute certiﬁcate. The Lean 4 separation
theorems are tractable in this arena (sets C and C′
), and the scaling demonstration leaves no architectural
escape hatch from the recurrent state.
The two recent attempts to scale nonlinear recurrence each took a diﬀerent concession. M2
RNN (Mishra
et al., 2026) trains nonlinear matrix-state recurrence at 7 B MoE in hybrid form (nonlinear-recurrent layers
interleaved with attention layers); ParaRNN (Danieli et al., 2025) trains nonlinear-recurrent LSTM and
GRU at 7 B by parallelizing the time loop via Newton iteration on a block-bidiagonal Jacobian. In both
cases the pure-recurrent setting was set aside rather than ﬁlled. This work takes the pure-recurrent route
and compares a delta-correcting instance directly with a raw-write instance under matched conditions.
M2
RNN already identiﬁes the same basic hardware fact: a nonlinear recurrence cannot be scanned across
time, so useful parallelism must come from batch and head parallelism rather than from the time axis. The
diﬀerence here is how far that principle is pushed and what it is used to test. M2
RNN promotes nonlinear
matrix recurrence mainly as a sparse ingredient in hybrid stacks; under the addressing-bottleneck hypothesis,
the pure setting may beneﬁt from many independent address programs rather than simply many value heads
— what we call address-program multiplicity. The CMA-reshaped raw-write comparator used here moves
M2
RNN into that same pure multi-programmed regime and is stable where the paper-default shape diverged.
This controls the broad pure-recurrent systems setting for comparing raw overwrite with delta correction; it
does not uniquely isolate the cause of divergence, since width, depth, and head count also changed.
Contributions
The contributions connect state-channel structure, practical training, and direct capability measurements.
1. Viability. E97 reaches an estimated 0.93 bits per byte on The Pile after 150.8 billion tokens of training
on a single eight-GPU workstation, with no cluster and no sequence parallelism. The throughput
route is width-axis multi-programming: 9,504 small bounded recurrent programs per token (216
heads × batch size 4 × depth 11), each a 32 × 32 matrix-state tile that ﬁts in registers, with the
time loop serial inside each program (Section 4). Pure-nonlinear-recurrent language models train
into this regime at this scale without a time-axis parallelization trick or attention hybridization.

ﬁxed-weight raw-write matrix RNN with row, column, or cell forget gates can match the Emender’s
delta-correcting update in one step (emender_m2rnn_one_step_resource_separation_embeds,
set C in Section 8), and that the gap persists for every ﬁnite k under composition on a con-
structed witness alphabet (emender_m2rnn_k_step_separation, set C′

2 Background
What a recurrent model can compute about its input, and how cheaply it can be trained to do so, both
turn on whether its state update is linear or nonlinear in the state — the hinge Section 1 introduced. This
section makes that hinge precise. It gives the structural test that sorts an architecture into one regime or
the other, the complexity ceiling the restricted linear models can face, and the word problem used to probe
learned state tracking. These three are the toolkit the architecture, systems, and results sections assume.
The stakes are the general ones of sequence modeling at scale, and one workload makes them concrete. Pange-
nomic sequence data runs to terabases per study (Liao et al., 2023) (Guarracino et al., 2023) (Garrison et al.,
2024), so modeling it at the byte level means ingesting trillions of tokens for any downstream operation,
which rules out routine use. Linear-recurrent byte-level foundation models were the ﬁrst attempt in this
regime and did not scale reliably. The Merrill–Petty–Sabharwal results (Merrill et al., 2024) give conditional
circuit-containment results for restricted linear SSMs under their transition and arithmetic assumptions.
Containment alone does not rule out non-solvable state tracking without an unproved complexity-class sepa-
ration. This motivated studying nonlinear-in-time updates, not a theorem that every pangenomic workload
requires them; the Emender of Section 3 is the construction evaluated here.

Linear-state and nonlinear-state recurrence
The recurrent language model lineage runs from Elman networks (Elman, 1990) and the LSTM (Hochreiter
& Schmidhuber, 1997) through the modern linear-recurrent variants: state-space models such as Mamba
and Mamba2 (Gu & Dao, 2024) (Dao & Gu, 2024), gated linear attention (Yang et al., 2023), and the delta
rule (Yang et al., 2024b). Many modern designs favor time-axis parallelism via linear-state updates, whereas
nonlinear-in-time recurrence generally resists that scan. This structural tradeoﬀ is not a universal historical
claim of declining expressive power across the lineage. Gated DeltaNet (Yang et al., 2024a) is the linear-
recurrent family the Section 6 8 M probes use as the linear baseline, and Gated DeltaNet-2 (Hatamizadeh
et al., 2026) — its split erase/write-gate extension — is the linear-recurrent baseline of the 1.3 B matched-
token comparison (Section 5). Mamba-3 (Lahoti et al., 2026) is reported by its authors to outperform GDN;
its compilation-heavy HPO does not ﬁt the Section 5 matched protocol, so it is left to future work.
A single explicit criterion classiﬁes recurrent architectures. A recurrent layer is linear-state if its update
can be written St = AtSt−1 + bt with At and bt depending on the current input xt only. Here the state is
expressed in vector coordinates; a matrix state may be vectorized for this criterion. Otherwise, if the previous
state St−1 appears nonlinearly in its own update equation, the layer is nonlinear-state. The distinction is
structural; it does not depend on whether the non-linearity is a tanh, an exponential, a tanh-of-product, or
a sigmoid.
Linear-state designs admit a chunkwise or scan-based parallel implementation along the time axis because
composing ST = AT ST −1 + bT = . . . unfolds into a product of inputs only. This is the reason linear-state
recurrence dominates the billion-parameter landscape. Mamba, Mamba2 (SSD), RetNet, GLA, DeltaNet,
Gated DeltaNet, RWKV-4/5/6/7, HGRN2, mLSTM, MinGRU/MinLSTM (Feng et al., 2024) and Griﬃn’s
RG-LRU (De et al., 2024) are all linear-state by this criterion. At ﬁxed precision and width, both state
regimes are ﬁnite-state recognizers. Circuit-complexity containment results for restricted linear SSMs require
additional arithmetic and transition assumptions (Merrill et al., 2024). Containment in TC0
alone does

The S5 state-tracking probe
The symmetric group S5 is the group of permutations of ﬁve items; it has 120 elements and is the smallest non-
solvable symmetric group. Its word problem is the following task: the model reads a sequence of permutations
— here, adjacent transpositions, the swaps of two neighboring items — one at a time, and after each one
must report the running composition of everything read so far, its preﬁx product. By Barrington’s theorem
(Barrington, 1986) this task is complete for the complexity class NC1
. Exact recognition over all words,
not ﬁnite-length accuracy, is the complexity-theoretic question; a failure to learn at training length does
not locate a model in a smaller circuit class. The solvable-group control S3 (permutations of three items, 6
elements) is included to factor out the part of diﬃculty that comes from preﬁx tracking per se rather than
from non-solvability.
A dynamics taxonomy of head-types: eigenvalue placement × saturation
The linear/nonlinear hinge above, and the choice of where a head’s state may decay, reﬂect, or oscillate, are
two independent axes of a single design grid rather than a list of unrelated tricks. Reading a head by the
dynamics of its per-token state map — not by the experiment that ﬁrst produced it — ﬁxes it as one point
in a two-axis grid:
1. Eigenvalue placement in the unit disk. Where the along-key eigenvalue of the per-token state transi-
tion sits in the complex unit disk. A real-positive eigenvalue is a decay — the stored value fades to-
ward zero along the key. A real-negative eigenvalue is a reﬂection that ﬂips sign each step (reflect);
reaching it is the lever that lets a recurrence track non-solvable group structure such as S5, within
the restricted transition analyses motivating this grid, not from aﬃnity alone. A complex-conjugate
pair is a rotation (rot) that oscillates the stored value. Real-positive and real-negative are the two
ends of the real diameter; complex placement walks oﬀ that diameter into the rest of the disk.
2. State map: linear or saturated. Whether the state update is left linear or passed through a bounded
saturating nonlinearity (canonically hardtanh; the -nonlin axis; the Emender uses the smooth
variant tanh). Saturation latches a driven slot and makes the head ﬁnite-state, the boundedness-
not-counting regime of Section 2.
The grid these two axes span names every Emender head-type (Figure 1). Real × linear at the positive end
is decay, which is vanilla Gated DeltaNet; at the negative end it is reflect, Gated DeltaNet permitted a
negative eigenvalue. Real × saturated is nonlin — the saturated delta-correcting head, historically tagged
E97, whose split-gated instance is the 1.3 B model of this paper (the earlier E88 instance carried the same
cell without the split gates). Complex × linear is rot, the complex-eigenvalue oscillator head. Complex ×
saturated is rot-nonlin, a nonlinear oscillator left to future work, included only once both axes have earned
it. The experiment tags E88, E97, E98, and E99 are retained throughout only as historical run identiﬁers;
the architecture vocabulary is this grid.
This grid makes the relationship to the linear-recurrent baseline exact rather than rhetorical.

Parameterization choices
E97 parameterizes the per-head decay dh in log space, following Mamba2 (Dao & Gu, 2024): each head
learns a scalar Alog and a bias δbias — the code names A_log and dt_bias — and forms the decay as
d = exp(− exp(Alog) · softplus(α(x) + δbias)), where α(x) is the input-dependent term and σ is again the
logistic sigmoid. Both decay parameters are exempt from weight decay, as is standard for such timescale
parameters, so the regularizer does not pull the learned decay toward a default. The recurrence is computed
in ﬂoat32 and cast back to the storage precision, and the tanh on the state uses the numerically stable form
tanh(z) = 2σ(2z) − 1 in the fused kernel to avoid overﬂow at large pre-activations. Three remaining choices
are deliberate omissions rather than additions: there is no output normalization inside the recurrent layer —
an ablation removed it for a 0.10-nat gain, and the wrapper’s block-level pre-norm plus ﬁnal norm suﬃce —
no short convolution on the input, and, for the E88 predecessor, no write gate. E97 adds the split erase/read
and write gates above; its output gate silu(g) still acts on the read alone.
The 1.3 B stack
The 1.3 B stack’s shape is not arbitrary. Ingredient (d) sets its character — many small heads rather than
one large matrix — and the numbers follow from it: each head’s 32 × 32 tile is small enough to live in
registers (Section 4), the head count H = 216 is large enough to give that many independent addressing
programs per layer, and eleven such layers — each followed by its SwiGLU MLP, the channel-mixer that
pairs with the token-mixing recurrence — reach the 1.3 B-class budget the paired comparison ﬁxes. The
exact values were set by the per-architecture search of Section 5, not by hand. At batch size 4, depth 11
and H = 216 multiply to 11 × 216 × 4 = 9{, }504 independent recurrent programs per token, each carrying
its own 32 × 32 state tile. The reference implementation and the fused Triton recurrence kernel that steps
these tiles are listed in the Appendix.

4 Multi-Programming and Systems
Multi-programming: the throughput-enabling design choice
Throughput comes from width, not from time. A linear recurrence earns its throughput on the time axis:
because St = AtSt−1 + bt unfolds into a product that depends on the inputs alone, the step-by-step loop
can be re-expressed as a preﬁx scan or a chunkwise matrix multiplication over the whole sequence at once.
A purely nonlinear recurrence cannot be rewritten that way without giving up the nonlinear update that
makes it expressive, so its time loop has to run serially. Multi-programming recovers the throughput on the
other axis. Rather than parallelize one recurrence along time, it replicates the recurrent computation across
many independent heads — each with its own small bounded matrix state — and harvests parallelism across
those heads, across state tiles, and across batch elements, while the time loop inside each head still runs one
step at a time.
The design choice is a decoupling: it frees throughput from the shape of the update. The recurrent body
must stay a bounded, register-resident per-step map at a ﬁxed matrix-state signature — that is what keeps
each head cheap — but it need not be compatible with an associative scan or a chunkwise WY product. The
14
cost is paid in per-head serial time, most exposed at long sequence lengths where the serial loop is longest;
the gain is that a nonlinear recurrence can keep a modern accelerator busy with no time-axis scan at all. The
route is agnostic to the update rule: both pure-nonlinear instances of the predecessor cohort — the Emender
and the raw-write M2
RNN-CMA — have geometries consistent with the same simple resource predicate.
The named Lean theorem RecurrentResourceFormalism.multiProgrammed_admits_m2rnn_and_emender
checks E88 and a diﬀerent M2
RNN proxy (depth 22, 70 heads, 256 state scalars/head), not the trained depth-
21, 370-head M2
RNN-CMA. The latter satisﬁes the four signature-ﬁeld conditions by inspection/arithmetic,
not by this exact-instance theorem (Section 8).
The 1.3 B Emender shape under multi-programming
A modern GPU exposes many independent streaming multiprocessors, each able to run a small program
out of its own registers and shared memory, and multi-programming is built to ﬁll them. E97, the 1.3 B
Emender instance, runs 216 such programs per layer per batch element — one per head — so at depth 11
and batch size 4 the layer stack issues 11 × 216 × 4 = 9{, }504 small independent recurrent programs per
token, each carrying its own 32×32 state tile small enough to live in registers. Keeping the accelerator busy
therefore asks nothing of parallelism along time: the parallelism across these programs is already enough to
ﬁll it. How fully it does is something the next section measures directly.
Measured throughput and utilization (CUDA only)
All throughput measurements and throughput-budget smokes reported here are CUDA-only; none establishes
correctness, performance, or parity on ROCm.
That the card stays busy is a claim about occupancy, and it is measured directly. Driving the 1.273 B E88
predecessor instance (batch 5, context 2048) through its own training path on a free NVIDIA RTX 6000 Ada
(48 GB) and sampling nvidia-smi once a second over a sustained post-warmup window, the GPU holds
median 100% utilization (mean 99.8%, minimum 96% across 133 samples) while drawing 97% of its 300
W power cap. The accelerator stays busy on width-axis multi-programming alone, with no time-axis scan.
Sustained throughput is 7,492 tokens/s (mean over steady 50-step windows; median 7,468); a slower sibling
GPU sustained 7,277 tokens/s, so the absolute ﬁgure is GPU-dependent at the few-percent level. E97 uses
the shared E88-derived Triton engine with split-edit inputs, but the E88 occupancy measurement is not an
E97 occupancy measurement.
At the literal 1.3 B pair geometry the two arms are measured head to head: on a leased RTX 6000 Ada under
identical conditions, E97-MLP sustains 3,211 tokens/s per GPU at batch 4 against 3,290 for GDN2-MLP
— a 0.976× tie — while using 28.9 GB against GDN2-MLP’s 35.7 GB peak memory, so E97 ﬁts batch 6
(4,470 tokens/s per GPU) where GDN2-MLP OOMs (experiments/preflight_100b/RESULTS.md). The
Emender’s edge at this scale is memory, not kernel speed; an earlier 1.26–1.56× speedup measured at small
grok-scale dims is not reproduced at 1.3 B.
This ﬁgure is occupancy, not peak arithmetic, and the two should not be run together. Model-FLOPs
utilization (MFU) — computed with the standard 6N convention against the card’s dense bf16 peak of 364
TFLOPS — is 15.7%, and that is a conservative lower bound: 6N counts only the weight-matmul FLOPs
and excludes the recurrence and gate operations, so the true MFU is slightly higher. High occupancy with
modest MFU is the expected proﬁle of a model bound by memory bandwidth and the serial recurrence rather
than by arithmetic: the GPU is busy nearly all of the time but converts about 16% of its peak FLOPs into
useful work.
What that throughput buys on the width axis is best read against a real time-axis kernel. A chunkwise
linear-scan baseline — Gated DeltaNet as implemented in the ﬂash-linear-attention (FLA) library (1.352 B,
the CMA-matched shape of the predecessor cohort), run on the same GPU, context, and time budget —
sustains 8,248 tokens/s at MFU 18.4% and the same near-100% occupancy. The E88 predecessor reaches ≈
91% of that linear-scan kernel’s tokens/s (7,492 / 8,248) without performing the sequential time-axis scan
at all. Width-axis multi-programming therefore recovers linear-scan-class throughput on the width axis. It
15
does not beat the chunked-scan kernel on raw tokens/s — FLA is about 10% faster here, at a slightly larger
parameter count and a diﬀerent batch — so the standing is parity-class, not superiority.
Fused Triton recurrence kernel
The forward kernel uses an internal [T,B,H,*] layout (time, batch, head, . . .) and dispatches one program
per (batch,head_block). The BLOCK_H parameter is autotuned over {1, 2, 4, 8, 16} because at H ≥ 256
per-program-per-head launch overhead dominates. The state tile lives in registers and shared memory for
the duration of the T-step loop. The following pieces are fused into the same Triton program: tanh/silu
activations on input projections, L2
normalization of q, k, the recurrent delta write, and the output gate.
Each fusion removes one to two torch.cuda launches per layer call; at depth 12 the aggregate saving is
approximately 50–60 ms per step.
Sparse-checkpoint backward
The forward kernel saves S only every K = 16 steps. The backward kernel processes one K-step segment
at a time: it forward-replays the K steps to rebuild per-step St−1 from the saved checkpoint, then walks
backward to apply the chain rule. The activation memory used by the kernel is T
K + 1 checkpoint slots
instead of T. At K = 16 this is approximately a 16× shrink, at the cost of a single extra forward pass per
segment.
Triton source portability; CUDA-only validation
The kernel is written in Triton (Tillet et al., 2019), a source-portability route rather than evidence of cross-
backend parity. The pair’s production runs, kernel throughput measurements, and expressivity throughput
smokes all ran on NVIDIA CUDA. No ROCm smoke or paired production artifact supports a correctness or
throughput claim here; no CUDA/ROCm parity is claimed. The successor E97 Frontier continuation is a
separate, single-arm line of work, not a portability benchmark for the E97/GDN2-MLP pair.
Distributed training
The 1.3 B-class result reported here was produced on a single eight-GPU workstation, with no cluster and
no sequence parallelism — both 150 B-token arms of Section 5 ran the same local DiLoCo recipe on that one
machine: eight independent islands (one per GPU), schedule-free AdamW (Defazio et al., 2024) inside each
island, and plain model-weight averaging across islands every τ = 250 local steps (outer learning rate 1.0,
momentum 0 — the conservative plain-average form of DiLoCo (Douillard et al., 2023)). Scaling the same
recipe past one machine pairs schedule-free AdamW per island with hierarchical local-SGD averaging: each
island is one node of 8 GPUs with intra-island DDP, and inter-island synchronization averages model weights
on the same τ = 250 cadence. The structural point holds either way: because the Emender’s parallelism is
across programs rather than along time, it does not need sequence parallelism to stay competitive at the 1.3
B-class — a simpliﬁcation relative to the chunked-scan implementations that linear-state recurrences rely
on.
A diﬀerent route would parallelize the time loop itself. ParaRNN (Danieli et al., 2025) does so with Newton’s
method on a block-bidiagonal Jacobian; we tried it on the tanh(dS + kδT
) map at 32 × 32 block size and
found it markedly slower in throughput than the multi-programmed Triton kernels above. Newton iteration
carries a data-dependent solve count per step, and its convergence on the bounded tanh map is unestablished,
whereas multi-programming is a single serial pass per head.

6 Expressivity Results
State-count headroom at 8 M parameters
State-tracking probes in this section run at 8 M parameter-matched scale (dim = 384, depth = 4, H = 32,
N = 32, schedule-free AdamW, 10K–20K steps per task, three seeds; GDN uses dim = 640 to match
parameter count). The S5 and S3 transition tables have information-theoretic ﬂoors of log2120 ≈ 6.9 bits
and log26 ≈ 2.6 bits; an 8 M-parameter model exceeds those ﬂoors by roughly seven orders of magnitude
in parameter bits and ﬁve in recurrent-state scalars per token. These counts rule out fewer-than-120-state
storage as the explanation, not a constrained-map expressivity or optimization bottleneck. A learning-failure
interpretation is a hypothesis consistent with this headroom and the 1.3 B parallel-decay boundary result,
not a consequence of the lookup theorem (Open item F1, Section 8). The probes measure what SGD ﬁnds
under the stated no-tuning protocol.

Sigma–delta conversion: quantization inside the loop
EXHIBIT. A ﬁrst-order sigma–delta modulator accumulates an analog sample and feeds back a quantized
output (Candy & Temes, 1992):
at = rt + xt, qt = Q(at), rt+1 = at − qt.
Here rt is the integrator residue, xt the injected sample, and the ﬁxed state map is F(r, x) = r+x−Q(r+x).
For a one-bit converter, Q(a) selects one of two output levels: a hard, discontinuous nonlinearity rather than
E97’s smooth saturation. The nonlinear result is fed back into the next state, not merely placed in an
output readout. Such streaming error-feedback loops are implemented directly in converter circuitry; they
need no large learned processor to execute the update. OPEN: the duality suggests testing a GDN surrogate
near quantizer thresholds over long streams, where residual errors can change later bits. A nonlinear state
update represents this feedback directly; a linear residue integrator with an output-only quantizer omits
the feedback dependence. No GDN lower bound follows: ﬁnite-resolution states, alternative encodings, or a
diﬀerent error metric may make a linear-state surrogate adequate. Predicting ﬁltered signal quality is also
weaker than predicting the exact bitstream.

Theorem set A: ﬁnite-state ceiling and S5 tracker
What to take from set A: at ﬁxed width and precision the Emender is a ﬁnite-state machine, and the S5 word
problem it is tested on is a concrete, fully speciﬁed ﬁnite-state target.
• Finite-state ceiling at ﬁxed precision. At ﬁxed width and precision, every online recognizer of this kind
has only ﬁnitely many distinct states (S5Witness.fixed_precision_state_space_finite). This bounds
its online memory to ﬁnitely many states; it does not prove strict containment below NC1
.
• S5 word problem. The target is pinned down exactly in Lean. The group S5 has 120 elements (S5Witn

reads a word of transpositions and reports the running product is a 120-state recognizer (S5Tracker.reco
gnizer_state_count) whose state composes correctly as tokens are appended (S5Tracker.run_append).
A bridge lemma conﬁrms the Lean tracker agrees with the Python evaluation harness on every input (S5Tr

run map is surjective (S5Inseparability.run_surjective, via Mathlib’s Equiv.Perm.mclosure_swap_c
astSucc_succ).
• Capacity lower bound (recognizer ﬂoor). Any ﬁnite-state recognizer with a state decoder that reproduces
the tracker on every input word needs at least 120 states (S5Inseparability.capacity_lower_bound_120);
equivalently, no recognizer with fewer than 120 states can realize it under any decoder (S5Inseparabili

with too few states admits two words it cannot tell apart that the tracker does distinguish (S5Insepara

9 Related Work
Ancestry: the fast-weight line on delta correction
The Emender update belongs to a long line that treats memory as a structure to correct rather than
to reﬁll. The earliest formulation is Widrow and Hoﬀ’s least-mean-square rule (Widrow & Hoﬀ, 1960),
which writes the error between target and current output back into the weight vector of an adaptive linear
element. Schmidhuber recast the same idea in a recurrent setting under the name fast-weight programmers
(Schmidhuber, 1992): a slow controller emits keys and values that update a fast-weight matrix online, with
the update being a correction against what the matrix currently predicts at the addressed slot. Schlag,
Irie, and Schmidhuber made the bridge to modern attention explicit (Schlag et al., 2021), showing that
linear-transformer kernels with cumulative outer-product updates are fast-weight programmers with a raw
additive write, and proposing the delta-rule write as a stability and capacity ﬁx. DeltaNet (Yang et al.,
2024b) carried the delta rule into a parallelizable linear-recurrent language model, demonstrating the rule
at billion-parameter scale in the linear-state regime. The Emender extends this line with three properties
the linear instantiations forfeit: a nonlinear matrix state, the saturation latching of Section 3 ingredient (c),
which motivates persistent binding under the conditional slot-level hold result (λ ≥ 1 plus a write budget),
not an achieved production-default property; and width-axis parallelism via multi-programming that recovers
throughput without time-axis linearization.
Linear-state recurrent language models
The dominant comparison cohort is the family of linear-state billion-parameter recurrent LMs surveyed in
Section 2: Mamba (Gu & Dao, 2024) and Mamba2 (Dao & Gu, 2024) (selective SSMs); RetNet (Sun et al.,
2023) (decayed linear attention); GLA (Yang et al., 2023) (gated linear attention); DeltaNet (Yang et al.,
2024b) (delta rule, linear in S); Gated DeltaNet (Yang et al., 2024a) (gated delta rule); RWKV-4/5/6/7 (Peng
et al., 2023) (Peng et al., 2025) (linear-state WKV / generalized delta rule with linear state); HGRN2 (Qin
et al., 2024) (gated linear RNN with state expansion); MinGRU/MinLSTM (Feng et al., 2024) (input-only
gates, linear scan); mLSTM/xLSTM-7B (Beck et al., 2025) (covariance update); Griﬃn/RecurrentGemma
(De et al., 2024) (RG-LRU, often hybridized with local attention). Circuit-containment results for restricted
SSM families require their stated transition and arithmetic assumptions (Merrill et al., 2024); aﬃnity alone
is not a universal complexity classiﬁcation. The empirical panel in Section 6 directly tests Gated DeltaNet

Formal gaps: G1–G3 and F1
G1 — no per-token circuit-size or depth lower bound. S5GDNBridge.gdn_s5_realization_requires_pe

G2 — state-dependent gates are unmodeled. CompositionalDepthDuality.gdn_state_channel_depth_bo

G3 — constrained aﬃne/reﬂection realizability is open. Whether the split-gated decay-minus-rank-one tran-

matrix, so the formal family must not be silently identiﬁed with scalar-decay production restrictions.
F1 — the nonlinear recurrent tracker is not realized by the lookup artifact. NDMRealizesS5.emender_

11 Conclusion
The duality gives the paper’s structural distinction: E97 executes a nonlinear state update at every step,
whereas an input-only-scheduled GDN state fold remains aﬃne in its initial state (Section 8). Width-axis
multi-programming makes the nonlinear route practical at 1.3 B; E97 trains through 150.8 B Pile tokens on
one eight-GPU workstation with an estimated training-log 0.93 bpb. Its parameter-matched, CMA-selected
GDN2-MLP control occupies the same training-loss band at matched tokens (Section 5); the current pair’s
held-out evaluation is still unavailable.

12 Testable Predictions
The results above suggest concrete predictions, most of them testable at the next scale step or under modest
additional training. Each is stated as a claim with its scope and the observation that would falsify it, so
that later training rounds can settle them.
• Width-axis multi-programming scales beyond 1.3 B without throughput collapse. The multi-
programmed substrate harvests throughput from many small bounded heads rather than from time-
axis linearization; the per-head register/SRAM working set is independent of total parameter count,
so the recipe is expected to survive scale-up to the 3–7 B band. Falsiﬁed if per-token throughput
degrades faster than the parameter count grows, beyond the kernel-arithmetic baseline.
• The Emender’s state-tracking advantage persists at scaled training budgets. The Section 6 S5 and S3
gaps are at the 8 M parameter-matched probe scale under matched no-tuning, and the iterated-map
41
separation is at grok scale. At longer training-token budgets at 1.3 B and beyond, the within-PNR
ordering and the Emender-vs-GDN2 gap on state-tracking probes should hold qualitatively — the
completed expressivity suite of Section 6 is the ﬁrst direct test, on the trained 150 B pair. The
predicted S5 ordering already fails on this pair under the tested protocols; the modular-quadratic
result supports only a task-dependent version. Further scale/budget persistence is falsiﬁed if that
map gap closes under a common optimization protocol.
• Emender ﬁne-tuned on reasoning benchmarks separates from GDN2-MLP on tasks requiring multi-
step state-tracking. The Section 6 QA panels at the predecessor cohort’s training budget sit below
the threshold where reasoning benchmarks diﬀerentiate. After targeted ﬁne-tuning on a reasoning
suite (BIG-Bench Hard, ReCLor, FOLIO, multi-step object tracking), the Emender is expected to
separate from the linear baseline speciﬁcally on items where multi-step preﬁx tracking is load-bearing
(object tracking, formal fallacies, multi-step entailment). Falsiﬁed if E97 and GDN2-MLP remain
within standard error on state-tracking-shaped subsets after matched ﬁne-tuning.
• PNR update-rule variants are per-step-body edits under the multi-programmed kernel. The fused
forward/backward Triton kernel (Section 4) is parameterized by the update map and the gating.
A diﬀerent bounded PNR update rule at the same matrix-state signature should be expressible
by editing the per-step body, without satisfying an associative-scan or chunkwise-WY compatibility
constraint and with the multi-programmed time loop unchanged. Falsiﬁed if a representative variant
(e.g., decoupled erase/write gates in the style of Gated DeltaNet-2 (Hatamizadeh et al., 2026), or a
higher-order delta with two-step memory) requires kernel rewrites beyond the per-step body.
• Emender combined with hybrid attention should outperform either alone. The Section 6 hybrid-
degradation result (Emender/GDN AABB underperforms either pure family on state tracking at
8 M) is read here as a property of the Emender/GDN pairing speciﬁcally. The prediction is that
an Emender-with-attention hybrid (Emender layers interleaved with attention layers, in the style of
M2
RNN’s hybrid conﬁguration) outperforms both pure Emender and pure attention on combined
state-tracking and long-range mixing benchmarks at matched parameter and token budgets. Falsiﬁed
if the hybrid underperforms either constituent at matched budget on the combined benchmark.
• A broadened CMA-ES sweep places the head-count interior optimum above the predecessor’s H =
370. The predecessor-cohort searches (Section B) arrived at H = 370 for E88 after successive bound
repositionings against persistent upward pressure from the optimizer. The conjecture is that the
same fairness search under broader bounds, subject only to the kernel-occupancy ceiling of the multi-
programmed substrate, returns an interior optimum at or above that band on the Emender at 1.3
B (E97’s own search selected H = 216 within its conﬁgured bounds); H ∼ 1000 is the next bracket
worth testing. Falsiﬁed if a broadened CMA-ES sweep under matched per-token compute returns
an interior optimum at or below the current selection.
Some of these will falsify; the falsiﬁcations are the next round of evidence.
13 Future Work
The results above leave several directions open. The ﬁrst few bear directly on the empirical comparison
of Section 5 and Section 6 the formal targets that follow are the open ends of the Lean development in
Section 8.
A stronger raw-write baseline
The within-class ordering reported here — the Emender ahead of the raw-write update M2
RNN-CMA at
the 8 M probes — is conditional on the no-tuning probe protocol, and the raw-write family is not a 1.3
B arm (Section 1). One contrast in the current evidence stays entangled because of that: the gap could
be a genuine diﬀerence in what the two update rules express, or it could be the residue of a raw-write
conﬁguration that received less eﬀective optimization. The way to separate the two is a stronger raw-write
42
baseline — a matched state-tracking search on the raw-write family at the 8 M probe shape, and a 1.3 B
raw-write arm once its kernels run on this stack — which distinguishes the conditional reading ("under the
eﬀort used here") from the family-level reading ("under any matched optimization"). It is the sharpest single
experiment for ﬁxing where the Emender-versus-raw-write gap actually sits, and Section 10 names it as the
load-bearing follow-up.
Scale beyond 1.3 B
The matched-token loss parity is measured at the 1.3 B class on The Pile, at a single 30-GPU-day training
extent per arm. This is the named scale limitation of Section 10: whether the parity holds at larger parameter
counts, and whether the state-tracking separation grows or vanishes with scale, is open. The load-bearing
experiment is a matched 3–7 B E97/GDN2-MLP pair under the same recipe; the single-arm E97 513 B-token
Frontier continuation that exists cannot answer a paired question, and a corpus move (e.g. to a license-clean
stream) would need its own matched pair. Section 12 states the throughput-scaling form of this question as
a falsiﬁable prediction.
Additional seeds and architecture-internal revalidation
Each 1.3 B run is a multi-week trajectory per architecture, so additional seeds at this band are a multi-
week investment apiece; the 1.3 B comparison is single-seed per architecture for that reason, and a seed-
averaged repeat is open. Several architecture-internal choices in the Emender — the output gate, the state
non-linearity (tanh versus linear), and the decay parameterization (simple sigmoid versus Mamba2-style
log-space) — tie on loss at small scale; revalidating each at 1.3 B is open.
A capacity bound on the S5 generators themselves
The k-step separation proved in Section 8 (emender_m2rnn_k_step_separation, set C′
) shows that for every
ﬁnite k the gap between the delta-correcting and raw-write updates persists, but it runs on a constructed
two-dimensional witness alphabet rather than on the S5 generators. The natural next-paper target is a
capacity bound stated directly on the S5 generator alphabet: for any ﬁxed-weight raw-write recurrence at
state dimension d, an explicit length T(d) past which tracking the S5 cosets becomes unreachable, while the
Emender at the same d still tracks them. That bound needs a bounded-precision raw-write class and Merrill-
style state-counting lower bounds not yet available in Mathlib. The Section 6 length-extrapolation curves on
parity, ﬁnite-state tracking, and the modular counter show positive but non-monotone gaps. For parity the
gap peaks near 0.359 at T = 320, then narrows to about 0.337 at T = 500. These are retained-advantage
observations, not evidence of the proposed S5 capacity bound or of a uniformly widening gap.
A partial order on PNR update rules
The ordering established here, delta-correcting above raw-write, is a noncontainment witness between two
matched-signature update families, not a proved strict set-inclusion order on the PNR class (emender_m2rn
n_one_step_resource_separation_embeds, set C in Section 8). Whether that relation extends to a partial
order over the broader PNR space — whether antisymmetry and transitivity hold across the full space, under
an explicit common resource class — is open. The horizon is the maximal element: which PNR update rule
carries the highest one-step expressive power for its compute class. The RecurrentResourceFormalism,
the Lean signature in which the Section 8 separations are stated, is the tool for climbing that order. More
ﬂexible bounded update rules are the natural rungs, among them split erase/write variants in the style of
Gated DeltaNet-2 (Hatamizadeh et al., 2026); placing them in the order is open work.
Lifting the latching theorems to the whole layer
The Section 8 latching set establishes the slot-level half of the Emender primitive: three theorems covering
saturation insensitivity, default hold under a bounded delta, and release on a suﬃcient counter-delta. Two
formal targets sit on top of it. The ﬁrst is a formalism reﬁnement — an architecture-level lift that promotes

Per-architecture CMA-ES protocol
All three 1.3 B-class architectures (Emender, M2
RNN-CMA, Gated DeltaNet) received independent CMA-
ES (Hansen et al., 2003) hyperparameter and shape search over the same six knobs: width, depth, head count,

ﬁxed wall-clock per candidate, identical ﬁtness rule of mean training nats over a ﬁxed late-training window.
When CMA-ES drifted onto a conﬁgured-range edge, the range was repositioned and search continued,
applied identically across the three architectures. The per-family winner shapes in the table above carry into
the Section 6 expressivity probes and Section 8 formal analysis unchanged. The concurrent M2
RNN paper
(Mishra et al. (Mishra et al., 2026), section 5.2) holds width, depth, optimizer, learning rate, weight decay
and gradient clipping uniform across compared architectures and varies only the sequence-mixing block, a
fair-by-uniformity protocol that does not give each architecture its own best-tuning.
The per-architecture search was adopted to avoid the undisclosed HPO budgets common in nearby work
and the contradictory within-class results that follow from them. It also functioned as a measurement. On
the Emender, CMA-ES repeatedly pushed head count up against whatever ceiling the bounds set, and each
range repositioning revealed the same pressure on the next iteration. The ﬁnal H = 370 is the interior
optimum reached after the bounds were placed far enough out that CMA-ES stopped against open ground.
A search conﬁgured for cross-architecture fairness thus placed its optimum at high head count without being
tuned toward it: throughput comes from many small, independently addressed recurrent programs.

C Held-out bits-per-byte measurement (predecessor cohort)
This appendix is the evidentiary backbone of the held-out descriptive closeness reported in Section B: it
documents exactly how the held-out bits-per-byte ﬁgures are measured and places them in a landscape of
reference models and classical compressors. Every number is measured by the eval harness described below;
none is re-ﬁtted. The measurement code, slice manifests, and re-exported checkpoints are public (see the

53
Tokenizer-invariant held-out bpb
So that models with diﬀerent tokenizers are comparable on identical bytes, held-out bpb is computed from
each model’s real summed negative log-likelihood over the slice and the slice’s real UTF-8 byte count:
bpb = (total NLLnats/ ln 2)/total UTF-8 bytes.
Each model uses its own tokenizer, and the denominator is the shared byte count, so bytes/token is a measured
per-tokenizer quantity (4.00 for the GPT-NeoX BPE, 3.82 for our p50k_base runs on the canonical slice)
— not the ﬁxed bytes/token = 3.92 constant used for the train-loss curve in Section 5. The held-out and
train-loss ﬁgures are therefore distinct objects: they diﬀer in normalization as well as in the data scored.
Every token is scored once under a sliding window (context 2048, stride 1024) with up to 2047 tokens of
left context, the standard ﬁxed-length-model perplexity recipe; out-of-distribution anchors GPT-2 XL and
OPT-1.3B run at context 1024 (GPT-2 XL position limit). Pipeline: scripts/measure_pile_bpb.py (open
models) and scripts/measure_pile_bpb_elman.py (our models, in-harness forward).
Algorithm 8 states the scoring order behind the held-out measurement. The denominator counts the shared
source bytes once; overlapping context is not rescored. Correct schedule-free weight recovery and the ﬁrst-
block sanity check precede reporting, and a failure is not a numeric measurement.
Algorithm 8 — tokenizer-invariant held-out evaluation.
Input: sha-verified slice bytes, model, tokenizer, window/stride
1. Load the bound inference weights; apply required y-mode recovery.
2. Tokenize the slice with this model’s tokenizer.
3. Run the first-block sanity gate; stop on failure.
4. Slide the context window; mask already scored target positions.
5. Sum next-token negative log-likelihood on newly scored positions.
6. Divide total NLL by log(2) and by the slice’s UTF-8 byte count.
Return: bits per byte, token/byte counts, slice and weight identities
