Emending Nonlinear Recurrence
Erik Garrison
erik.garrison@gmail.com
Poietic PBC / UTHSC, Memphis, TN, USA
Abstract
Nonlinear-state recurrence grows executed compositional depth in time: E97
applies one tanh-of-affine state update per token. In the dual regime, a linear-
state GDN fold with a state-independent parameter schedule remains affine in
its initial state, with zero nonlinear temporal depth. Lean 4 artifacts establish
this structural duality, matched-signature delta-versus-raw-write separation, and
an 𝑆5 routing bridge; they do not lower-bound minimal circuit depth or prove a
full-stack impossibility. Width-axis multi-programming makes the nonlinear route
practical: a 1.3 B Emender trains through 150.8 B Pile tokens on one eight-
GPU workstation, while a parameter-matched, CMA-selected GDN2-MLP control
occupies the same training-loss band. Multi-seed small-scale iterated-map probes
show a depth-wall signature that is replicated on this trained 1.3 B pair: modular-
quadratic terminal accuracy is 99.96% versus 76.09% at trained length, and
76.07% versus 53.36% at eight times that length. This is a single-seed, common-
cap comparison with unequal realized training, not invariant extrapolation. The
negative transfer is explicit: “The 8M S5 separation does NOT replicate at 1.3B
on this pair.” Repeated nonlinear maps in feedback, optimization, reservoirs,
and dynamics define the applications scope, not demonstrated deployments. For
TMLR, the contribution is correctness-first: inspectable Lean statements, artifact-
derived results, disclosed negatives, and numbered gaps, including recurrent
tracker realization and constrained affine-channel realizability.

1 Introduction
A recurrent sequence model reads its input one step at a time, folding each token into an internal
state that summarizes everything seen so far. How such a model can be trained, and how fast,
turns almost entirely on a single design choice: how that state is updated from one step to the
next. Over the past several years the field has converged on updates that are linear in the state.
A linear update can be re-expressed as a parallel scan over the whole sequence at once instead
of a step-by-step loop, and that re-expression is what lets recurrent models reach the training
throughput of attention on modern hardware. State-space models and gated linear-attention
architectures — Mamba and its successors [1], [2], [3], gated linear attention [4], RWKV [5],
and the delta rule and its gated form [6], [7] — now stand alongside the transformer as the
efficient backbone of choice for long sequences.
1
This efficiency is bought with a structural concession. A state update that is linear in the state
can be parallelized, but it also bounds what the state can compute as the sequence grows:
restricted linear-state recurrence can have limited circuit complexity under explicit arithmetic
and architectural assumptions; the prefix products of a non-solvable group are a canonical
diagnostic [8], [9]. A direct way to add nonlinear temporal computation is to pass the state
through a nonlinearity at every step. But a nonlinear-in-time update breaks the parallel scan
and forces the time loop to run serially, and serial training at billion-parameter scale was
presumed too slow to be practical. The field’s response has been to route around the obstruction
rather than meet it: parallelize the time axis by other means, such as Newton iteration on
a block-bidiagonal Jacobian [10], or interleave recurrent layers with attention so the hardest
state-tracking work falls to the attention [11], [12], [13], [14].
Two questions are left open by this detour. First, whether a purely nonlinear-in-time recurrence
— no parallel scan, no attention to lean on — can be trained to competitive scale at all, on
a realistic training budget. Second, once it can, whether the particular form of the nonlinear
update rule makes any difference to what the trained model can do. Neither had been tested
directly, because the pure setting had been set aside as the expensive case to avoid rather than
the case to measure.
Here we show that it can be trained, and that the form of the update does matter. A purely
nonlinear recurrent language model — E97, the current 1.3 B-class instance of the Emender
family — made trainable by running many small recurrent programs in parallel across the
network’s width rather than across time, trains through 150.8 billion tokens on The Pile [15]
using a single eight-GPU workstation, with no cluster and no sequence parallelism, reaching an
estimated 0.93 bits per byte (Section 5). Its linear-recurrent baseline — GDN2-MLP, a Gated
DeltaNet-2 [16] mixer followed by the same SwiGLU MLP, selected by the same CMA-ES [17]
search protocol at a parameter count within 0.01% — trains on literally matched tokens under
the same 2k-chunk Pile recipe and lands in the same loss-versus-tokens band: at the matched
150.79-billion-token cutoff the smoothed loss gap between the two arms is 0.010 nats/token,
with the sign of the gap depending on the averaging window (Section 5). The 1.3 B result is
therefore a replication across the update rule and tuning regime, not across scale: both arms sit
in the same 1.3 B-class parameter band, and whether the separation or the loss parity survives
at 7 B and beyond is named openly as a limitation in Section 10. On bulk training-log loss the
two update families are operationally tied, so language-modeling loss does not separate them.
Their differences emerge instead in state tracking. At 8 M scale, the delta-correcting update
learns the 𝑆5 prefix-product task more efficiently than the raw-write and GDN baselines under
the tested protocols. At 1.3 B, the modular-quadratic probe reproduces a depth-wall signature,
but the 𝑆5 ordering does not transfer (Section 6). The Lean core proves matched-signature one-
step separation, exact non-equality at every finite 𝑘 on a constructed witness, and a 120-state
recognizer floor. Its generator-table lookup construction is not a proof that the tanh recurrence
implements the full tracker; this missing trajectory bridge is Open item F1 (Section 8). Neither
the duality nor the cited complexity results establish a blanket linear-state 𝑆5 impossibility.
Throughput on the width axis comes from many small recurrent programs running side by side,
each stepping its time loop serially, which is what makes the serial recurrence practical at scale.
The architecture is a residual stack of recurrent layers. Each layer pairs a matrix-state memory
with a write rule that has two halves. Delta correction is the first half. The layer reads what the
memory predicts at the addressed slot, computes the prediction error, and writes the correction.
2
Memory overwrites a stale binding when a later token addresses the same slot with a different
value. Tanh-with-latching is the second half. Under the hypotheses of theorem set F (Section 8),
a saturated slot resists perturbations, keeps its sign below a write threshold, and releases
under a sufficient counter-delta. These are conditional slot-level guarantees; the hold theorem
EmenderLatching.emender_latch_holds_by_default assumes 𝜆 ≥ 1, not the production decay
below one. Persistent, revisable memory is the design motivation, not a proved default behavior
of the trained stack. An emender is one such layer; the Emender is the architecture family
obtained by stacking emender layers; E97 — the saturated delta-correcting cell extended with
split erase/read and value-write gates — is the 1.3 B instance evaluated throughout this paper,
and E88 is the developmental predecessor instance that preceded it (Section APPENDIX A).
A Lean 4 trusted core is the spine of the argument. The state-channel duality proves that
E97 appends one nonlinear update per step, while a GDN fold with a state-independent
schedule stays affine in its initial state. It also proves matched-signature delta-versus-raw-write
separation, split-gated relations, slot-wise latching guarantees under stated hypotheses, and the
𝑆5 recognizer floor (Section 8). These are structural facts, not application-level approximation
lower bounds. The empirical work asks what SGD finds: at parameter-matched 8 M scale the
Emender reaches 0.79 on 𝑆5 against 0.36 for GDN and 0.22 for raw-write (Section 6), whereas
the current 1.3 B pair supplies a positive iterated-map result and a negative group-state result.
Width-axis multi-programming makes this theory-to-learning comparison practical.
Delta correction is one response; hybrids are another
The recurrent state-tracking problem admits more than one architectural response. Hybrid
architectures interleave recurrent layers with attention layers and recover much of what
attention-free recurrence loses on state tracking and long-range mixing. M²RNN [11], OLMo-
Hybrid [12], Titans [13], and Griffin [14] are recent instances, and the limit-of-pure-recurrence
story is documented by Merrill, Petty and Sabharwal [8]. Delta correction is a different response.
It does not add cross-token mixing; it changes what the recurrent state writes at each step
from a raw additive overwrite to a correction against the existing slot. The two responses are
complementary, not rival: a hybrid stack can use any update rule in its recurrent layers, and
delta correction is well-defined wherever a matrix-state recurrent layer is.
We evaluate delta correction in the attention-free, time-serial recurrent arena, the pure
nonlinear recurrent (PNR) setting in the sense of “no cross-token attention mechanism”; gating,
projections, normalizations and other intra-token nonlinear operations are permitted and load-
bearing (Section 3 ingredient (d) and Section 5 on gradient conditioning). The PNR arena is
where the contrast is cleanest (a delta-vs-raw-write contrast in the specified update-resource
class, Section 8). Set D uses synthetic state-pass charges, excluding actual dense 𝐻𝑊 and
feature-network costs; it is not an actual matched-compute certificate. The Lean 4 separation
theorems are tractable in this arena (sets C and C′), and the scaling demonstration leaves no
architectural escape hatch from the recurrent state.
The two recent attempts to scale nonlinear recurrence each took a different concession. M²RNN
[11] trains nonlinear matrix-state recurrence at 7 B MoE in hybrid form (nonlinear-recurrent
layers interleaved with attention layers); ParaRNN [10] trains nonlinear-recurrent LSTM and
GRU at 7 B by parallelizing the time loop via Newton iteration on a block-bidiagonal Jacobian.
In both cases the pure-recurrent setting was set aside rather than filled. This work takes the
3
pure-recurrent route and compares a delta-correcting instance directly with a raw-write instance
under matched conditions.
M²RNN already identifies the same basic hardware fact: a nonlinear recurrence cannot be
scanned across time, so useful parallelism must come from batch and head parallelism rather
than from the time axis. The difference here is how far that principle is pushed and what it
is used to test. M²RNN promotes nonlinear matrix recurrence mainly as a sparse ingredient
in hybrid stacks; under the addressing-bottleneck hypothesis, the pure setting may benefit
from many independent address programs rather than simply many value heads — what we
call address-program multiplicity. The CMA-reshaped raw-write comparator used here moves
M²RNN into that same pure multi-programmed regime and is stable where the paper-default
shape diverged. This controls the broad pure-recurrent systems setting for comparing raw
overwrite with delta correction; it does not uniquely isolate the cause of divergence, since width,
depth, and head count also changed.
Contributions
The contributions connect state-channel structure, practical training, and direct capability
measurements.
1. Viability. E97 reaches an estimated 0.93 bits per byte on The Pile after 150.8 billion tokens of
training on a single eight-GPU workstation, with no cluster and no sequence parallelism. The
throughput route is width-axis multi-programming: 9,504 small bounded recurrent programs
per token (216 heads × batch size 4 × depth 11), each a 32 × 32 matrix-state tile that fits in
registers, with the time loop serial inside each program (Section 4). Pure-nonlinear-recurrent
language models train into this regime at this scale without a time-axis parallelization trick
or attention hybridization.
2. Power separation within the formalized resource class: for the matched-signature update
family studied here, delta correction supplies a mixed-key step absent from the fixed-
right raw-write class in a shared synthetic state-pass accounting model. A Lean 4
trusted core machine-verifies that no fixed-weight raw-write matrix RNN with row,
column, or cell forget gates can match the Emender’s delta-correcting update in
one step (emender_m2rnn_one_step_resource_separation_embeds, set C in Section 8),
and that the gap persists for every finite 𝑘 under composition on a constructed
witness alphabet (emender_m2rnn_k_step_separation, set C′). The cost comparison
(emender_m2rnn_flop_class_equiv, set D) uses coarse state-pass charges, excluding actual
dense right-multiplication 𝐻𝑊 and feature-network costs; it does not certify matched
empirical compute. This is a representability statement inside the formalized fixed-weight
raw-write resource class, not a claim of general or learned-weight superiority. Empirically
the delta-vs-raw-write contrast is a matched-budget learning-efficiency gap rather than a
categorical impossibility: at matched no-tuning budget raw-write under-reaches the delta
update’s 𝑆5 length generalization, and even at a tuned best-effort budget it does not catch
up (Section 6). The predicted ordering already appears at the 8 M parameter-matched probe
shape where the Section 6 state-count argument gives large storage headroom: the Emender
reaches 0.79 on the 𝑆5 word problem against 0.22 for raw-write M²RNN-CMA (Section 6).
3. Paired 1.3 B comparison: loss does not distinguish the update rules; matched tokens do not
favor either. Two 1.3 B-class models — E97 (delta-correcting, nonlinear state) and GDN2-
MLP (linear-state, the strongest closely related recurrent baseline, CMA-ES-hardened under
4
the same search protocol) — train from scratch on the same 2k-chunk Pile recipe with
literally matched aggregate tokens (150.79 B) at a 0.01% parameter gap, and land in the
same loss-versus-tokens band (Section 5). At 480 M scale, four CMA-ES-tuned recurrent
families share one FLOPs-per-bit curve (Section 5). The contribution is the architectural
option plus the mechanism, not a training-speed win — bulk loss is a convergent null across
the tuned families.
4. Expressivity at 1.3 B: a task-dependent separation. On the trained pair itself, the modular-
quadratic probe separates terminal accuracy and length robustness under a pre-registered
common optimization cap (Section 6, Table 9). The 𝑆5 separation does not replicate: GDN2-
MLP is ahead in the long-horizon probe (Table 10). Realizability is not a guarantee of
learning; the two findings jointly delimit the theory-to-empirics transfer.
The 1.3 B pairing is E97-MLP (1,286,589,072 parameters; the Emender split-edit delta-
correcting update — a key-axis erase/read gate and a value-axis write gate around the bounded
delta correction, Section 3 — with a bias-free SwiGLU MLP per layer) versus GDN2-MLP
(1,286,713,448 parameters; the official-style Gated DeltaNet-2 [16] mixer with the same SwiGLU
MLP), both selected by per-architecture CMA-ES [17] search at the 1.3 B band on The Pile

machine’s hardware stack, and a local reimplementation would add an implementation-fidelity
confound — so it is carried at the 8 M probe scale and in the Lean separation instead (Section 6,
Section 8). The exact per-arm search provenance, budgets, and training identities are reported
in Section 5.

2 Background
What a recurrent model can compute about its input, and how cheaply it can be trained to
do so, both turn on whether its state update is linear or nonlinear in the state — the hinge
Section 1 introduced. This section makes that hinge precise. It gives the structural test that
sorts an architecture into one regime or the other, the complexity ceiling the restricted linear
6
models can face, and the word problem used to probe learned state tracking. These three are
the toolkit the architecture, systems, and results sections assume.
The stakes are the general ones of sequence modeling at scale, and one workload makes them
concrete. Pangenomic sequence data runs to terabases per study [18], [19], [20], so modeling
it at the byte level means ingesting trillions of tokens for any downstream operation, which
rules out routine use. Linear-recurrent byte-level foundation models were the first attempt in
this regime and did not scale reliably. The Merrill–Petty–Sabharwal results [8] give conditional
circuit-containment results for restricted linear SSMs under their transition and arithmetic
assumptions. Containment alone does not rule out non-solvable state tracking without an
unproved complexity-class separation. This motivated studying nonlinear-in-time updates, not
a theorem that every pangenomic workload requires them; the Emender of Section 3 is the
construction evaluated here.
Linear-state and nonlinear-state recurrence
The recurrent language model lineage runs from Elman networks [21] and the LSTM [22]
through the modern linear-recurrent variants: state-space models such as Mamba and Mamba2
[1], [2], gated linear attention [4], and the delta rule [6]. Many modern designs favor time-axis
parallelism via linear-state updates, whereas nonlinear-in-time recurrence generally resists that
scan. This structural tradeoff is not a universal historical claim of declining expressive power
across the lineage. Gated DeltaNet [7] is the linear-recurrent family the Section 6 8 M probes
use as the linear baseline, and Gated DeltaNet-2 [16] — its split erase/write-gate extension —
is the linear-recurrent baseline of the 1.3 B matched-token comparison (Section 5). Mamba-3
[3] is reported by its authors to outperform GDN; its compilation-heavy HPO does not fit the
Section 5 matched protocol, so it is left to future work.
A single explicit criterion classifies recurrent architectures. A recurrent layer is linear-state if
its update can be written ℎ𝑡 = 𝐴𝑡ℎ𝑡−1 + 𝑏𝑡 with 𝐴𝑡 and 𝑏𝑡 depending on the current input 𝑥𝑡
only. Otherwise, if the previous state ℎ𝑡−1 appears nonlinearly in its own update equation, the
layer is nonlinear-state. The distinction is structural; it does not depend on whether the non-
linearity is a tanh, an exponential, a tanh-of-product, or a sigmoid.
Linear-state designs admit a chunkwise or scan-based parallel implementation along the
time axis because composing ℎ𝑇 = 𝐴𝑇 ℎ𝑇−1 + 𝑏𝑇 = … unfolds into a product of inputs only.
This is the reason linear-state recurrence dominates the billion-parameter landscape. Mamba,
Mamba2 (SSD), RetNet, GLA, DeltaNet, Gated DeltaNet, RWKV-4/5/6/7, HGRN2, mLSTM,
MinGRU/MinLSTM [23] and Griffin’s RG-LRU [14] are all linear-state by this criterion. At
fixed precision and width, both state regimes are finite-state recognizers. Circuit-complexity
containment results for restricted linear SSMs require additional arithmetic and transition
assumptions [8]. Containment in TC0

Two nonlinear matrix-state designs (the Emender and M²RNN) therefore share the necessary
preconditions (matrix state, nonlinearity on the state, no attention, no linearization, no
hybridization) that define the pure-nonlinear-recurrent class introduced in Section 1 and differ
in one place: the per-step update rule on the matrix state. The Emender uses a delta-correcting
update; M²RNN uses a raw-write update; both are pure-nonlinear-recurrent instances. The
within-class observation in Section 1 is a task-dependent learning-efficiency ordering under
the reported budgets and per-architecture CMA-ES, not a general learnability theorem. The
Lean cost anchor RecurrentResourceFormalism.emender_m2rnn_flop_class_equiv establishes
only equivalence in a synthetic, coarse state-pass accounting model (Section 8 Theorem set D).
It excludes the actual dense right-multiplication 𝐻𝑊 and feature-network costs, charging a
state-sized atom rather than the arithmetic of that multiplication. It therefore does not certify
actual matched empirical compute.

The 𝑆5 state-tracking probe
The symmetric group 𝑆5 is the group of permutations of five items; it has 120 elements and is the
smallest non-solvable symmetric group. Its word problem is the following task: the model reads
a sequence of permutations — here, adjacent transpositions, the swaps of two neighboring items
— one at a time, and after each one must report the running composition of everything read so
far, its prefix product. By Barrington’s theorem [9] this task is complete for the complexity class
NC1
. Exact recognition over all words, not finite-length accuracy, is the complexity-theoretic
question; a failure to learn at training length does not locate a model in a smaller circuit class.
The solvable-group control 𝑆3 (permutations of three items, 6 elements) is included to factor
out the part of difficulty that comes from prefix tracking per se rather than from non-solvability.

A dynamics taxonomy of head-types: eigenvalue placement ×
saturation
The linear/nonlinear hinge above, and the choice of where a head’s state may decay, reflect, or
oscillate, are two independent axes of a single design grid rather than a list of unrelated tricks.
Reading a head by the dynamics of its per-token state map — not by the experiment that first
produced it — fixes it as one point in a two-axis grid:
1. Eigenvalue placement in the unit disk. Where the along-key eigenvalue of the per-token state
transition sits in the complex unit disk. A real-positive eigenvalue is a decay — the stored
value fades toward zero along the key. A real-negative eigenvalue is a reflection that flips sign
each step (reflect); reaching it is the lever that lets a recurrence track non-solvable group
structure such as 𝑆5, within the restricted transition analyses motivating this grid, not from
affinity alone. A complex-conjugate pair is a rotation (rot) that oscillates the stored value.
Real-positive and real-negative are the two ends of the real diameter; complex placement
walks off that diameter into the rest of the disk.
2. State map: linear or saturated. Whether the state update is left linear or passed through a
bounded saturating nonlinearity (canonically hardtanh; the -nonlin axis; the Emender uses
the smooth variant tanh). Saturation latches a driven slot and makes the head finite-state,
the boundedness-not-counting regime of Section 2.
The grid these two axes span names every Emender head-type (Figure 1). Real × linear at the

convolution on the input, and, for the E88 predecessor, no write gate. E97 adds the split erase/
read and write gates above; its output gate silu(𝑔) still acts on the read alone.

4 Multi-Programming and Systems
Multi-programming: the throughput-enabling design choice
Throughput comes from width, not from time. A linear recurrence earns its throughput on the
time axis: because ℎ𝑡 = 𝐴𝑡ℎ𝑡−1 + 𝑏𝑡 unfolds into a product that depends on the inputs alone,
the step-by-step loop can be re-expressed as a prefix scan or a chunkwise matrix multiplication
over the whole sequence at once. A purely nonlinear recurrence cannot be rewritten that way
without giving up the nonlinear update that makes it expressive, so its time loop has to run
serially. Multi-programming recovers the throughput on the other axis. Rather than parallelize
one recurrence along time, it replicates the recurrent computation across many independent
heads — each with its own small bounded matrix state — and harvests parallelism across those
heads, across state tiles, and across batch elements, while the time loop inside each head still
runs one step at a time.
The design choice is a decoupling: it frees throughput from the shape of the update. The
recurrent body must stay a bounded, register-resident per-step map at a fixed matrix-
state signature — that is what keeps each head cheap — but it need not be compatible
with an associative scan or a chunkwise WY product. The cost is paid in per-head
serial time, most exposed at long sequence lengths where the serial loop is longest; the
gain is that a nonlinear recurrence can keep a modern accelerator busy with no time-
axis scan at all. The route is agnostic to the update rule: both pure-nonlinear instances
of the predecessor cohort — the Emender and the raw-write M²RNN-CMA — have
geometries consistent with the same simple resource predicate. The named Lean theorem

The 1.3 B Emender shape under multi-programming
A modern GPU exposes many independent streaming multiprocessors, each able to run a small
program out of its own registers and shared memory, and multi-programming is built to fill
them. E97, the 1.3 B Emender instance, runs 216 such programs per layer per batch element —
one per head — so at depth 11 and batch size 4 the layer stack issues 11 × 216 × 4 = 9{, }504
small independent recurrent programs per token, each carrying its own 32 × 32 state tile small
enough to live in registers. Keeping the accelerator busy therefore asks nothing of parallelism
along time: the parallelism across these programs is already enough to fill it. How fully it does
is something the next section measures directly.
Measured throughput and utilization (CUDA only)
All throughput measurements and throughput-budget smokes reported here are CUDA-only;
none establishes correctness, performance, or parity on ROCm.
That the card stays busy is a claim about occupancy, and it is measured directly. Driving the
1.273 B E88 predecessor instance (batch 5, context 2048) through its own training path on a

on width-axis multi-programming alone, with no time-axis scan. Sustained throughput is 7,492
tokens/s (mean over steady 50-step windows; median 7,468); a slower sibling GPU sustained
7,277 tokens/s, so the absolute figure is GPU-dependent at the few-percent level. E97 uses the
shared E88-derived Triton engine with split-edit inputs, but the E88 occupancy measurement
is not an E97 occupancy measurement.
At the literal 1.3 B pair geometry the two arms are measured head to head: on a leased RTX

kernel speed; an earlier 1.26–1.56× speedup measured at small grok-scale dims is not reproduced
at 1.3 B.
This figure is occupancy, not peak arithmetic, and the two should not be run together. Model-

bound by memory bandwidth and the serial recurrence rather than by arithmetic: the GPU is
busy nearly all of the time but converts about 16% of its peak FLOPs into useful work.

Fused Triton recurrence kernel
The forward kernel uses an internal [T, B, H, *] layout (time, batch, head, …) and dispatches
one program per (batch, head_block). The BLOCK_H parameter is autotuned over {1, 2, 4, 8, 16}
because at 𝐻 ≥ 256 per-program-per-head launch overhead dominates. The state tile lives in
registers and shared memory for the duration of the 𝑇-step loop. The following pieces are fused
into the same Triton program: tanh/silu activations on input projections, 𝐿2
normalization of
𝑞, 𝑘, the recurrent delta write, and the output gate. Each fusion removes one to two torch.cuda
launches per layer call; at depth 12 the aggregate saving is approximately 50–60 ms per step.
Sparse-checkpoint backward
The forward kernel saves 𝑆 only every 𝐾 = 16 steps. The backward kernel processes one 𝐾-
step segment at a time: it forward-replays the 𝐾 steps to rebuild per-step 𝑆𝑡−1 from the saved
checkpoint, then walks backward to apply the chain rule. The activation memory used by the
kernel is 𝑇
𝐾 + 1 checkpoint slots instead of 𝑇. At 𝐾 = 16 this is approximately a 16 × shrink,
at the cost of a single extra forward pass per segment.
Triton source portability; CUDA-only validation
The kernel is written in Triton [28], a source-portability route rather than evidence of cross-
backend parity. The pair’s production runs, kernel throughput measurements, and expressivity
throughput smokes all ran on NVIDIA CUDA. No ROCm smoke or paired production artifact
supports a correctness or throughput claim here; no CUDA/ROCm parity is claimed. The
successor E97 Frontier continuation is a separate, single-arm line of work, not a portability
benchmark for the E97/GDN2-MLP pair.
Distributed training
The 1.3 B-class result reported here was produced on a single eight-GPU workstation, with
no cluster and no sequence parallelism — both 150 B-token arms of Section 5 ran the same
local DiLoCo recipe on that one machine: eight independent islands (one per GPU), schedule-
free AdamW [29] inside each island, and plain model-weight averaging across islands every 𝜏 =
250 local steps (outer learning rate 1.0, momentum 0 — the conservative plain-average form of
DiLoCo [30]). Scaling the same recipe past one machine pairs schedule-free AdamW per island
with hierarchical local-SGD averaging: each island is one node of 8 GPUs with intra-island DDP,
and inter-island synchronization averages model weights on the same 𝜏 = 250 cadence. The
structural point holds either way: because the Emender’s parallelism is across programs rather
than along time, it does not need sequence parallelism to stay competitive at the 1.3 B-class
— a simplification relative to the chunked-scan implementations that linear-state recurrences
rely on.

6 Expressivity Results
State-count headroom at 8 M parameters
State-tracking probes in this section run at 8 M parameter-matched scale (dim = 384, depth =
4, 𝐻 = 32, 𝑁 = 32, schedule-free AdamW, 10K–20K steps per task, three seeds; GDN uses dim
= 640 to match parameter count). The 𝑆5 and 𝑆3 transition tables have information-theoretic
floors of log2 120 ≈ 6.9 bits and log2 6 ≈ 2.6 bits; an 8 M-parameter model exceeds those floors
by roughly seven orders of magnitude in parameter bits and five in recurrent-state scalars per
token. These counts rule out fewer-than-120-state storage as the explanation, not a constrained-
map expressivity or optimization bottleneck. A learning-failure interpretation is a hypothesis
consistent with this headroom and the 1.3 B parallel-decay boundary result, not a consequence
of the lookup theorem (Open item F1, Section 8). The probes measure what SGD finds under
the stated no-tuning protocol.
Matched no probe-tuning, and the one selection asymmetry it does
not cover
The 8 M probe scale received no probe-specific hyperparameter search and no seed sweep for
any family in the comparison. The Emender ran on the default configuration carried down
from its 1.3 B stack (the E88-lineage default; the E97 instance of Section 5 postdates these
probes); M²RNN-CMA ran on the analogous default from its CMA-tuned reshape; GDN and
the M²RNN-paper shape ran on their respective published defaults. Each family is therefore
evaluated on the reasonable-defaults configuration it would arrive at without probe-targeted
optimization, rather than matched-after-HPO. With large state-count headroom and no probe-
specific tuning, the gaps are consistent with an inductive-bias explanation, but do not uniquely
identify it.

That matched-no-tuning condition controls for probe-specific effort; it does not control for one

ablation lineage ranked on language-modeling loss — the same objective GDN’s and M²RNN’s
authors used for their published defaults — with one exception: the state nonlinearity (tanh
versus a linear state) was descriptively close to the linear variant on language-modeling loss
and was kept on state-tracking grounds. That one component is load-bearing for these probes,
so the Emender’s configuration was settled partly on a proxy of the very quantity the probes
measure, whereas no baseline’s was. The asymmetry favors the Emender, and it bears on the
magnitude of the within-class 𝑆5 gap.
Two observations bound, but do not eliminate, that confound. Raw-write M²RNN under-reaches
even on solvable 𝑆3; GDN is stronger on 𝑆3 than 𝑆5. Neither observation proves the cause of

recurrent realizability theorem (Section 8, Open item F1). A matched state-tracking search on
each family at the 8 M shape would test the inductive-bias hypothesis and the magnitude of
the advantage (Section 13).

repeated nonlinear transformation of its carried state, the duality says that nonlinear state
computation cannot simply be dropped while leaving the same update in the same coordinates:

state recurrence is not optional; an alternative must supply an adequate representation or
approximation elsewhere. Whether a fixed-depth GDN-style stack can do so at acceptable width,
precision, latency, and horizon remains OPEN for these applications. Stable, fading-memory
and affine-representable cases are explicit exceptions to any blanket advantage claim. The

8 Formal Results
The claims in this section are checked by a machine rather than argued on paper. A trusted
Lean 4 [39] core built on Mathlib [40] carries the formal spine: each result is stated as a theorem
and compiled together with a proof of it. Trust rests on what those proofs are not allowed to
assume. The import closure of the ElmanProofs.PaperCore module — 17 project source files in
the recursive local closure — uses none of Lean’s escape hatches: no sorry, admit, added axiom,
opaque declarations, or native_decide. The gate is formal/lean/scripts/check_paper_core.sh;
compiling ElmanProofs.PaperCore additionally checks elaboration of the proofs. The refreshed
machine-extracted inventory is committed at paper/data/lean-trust-gate-theorems.json (857
named declarations, 723 user-written, 17 files; extraction source commit e58ae937), including
the split-gated, latching, duality, routing, Lipschitz, and E1H-definition modules. Both the gate
and extractor traverse the recursive local imports rather than a selected module allowlist. This
check covers project sources, not the axioms or source files of Lean and Mathlib dependencies.
Every result below is named by its exact theorem so a reader can open the source and find it.
What is proved here is realizability and representational scope; what a trained model actually
learns under SGD is the separate empirical question of Section 5 and Section 6.
The formal spine starts with the compositional-depth duality and routing bridge (set H below),
which explain the lens used in Section 6 and Section 8. The remaining sets specify exact lookup,
recognizer count, matched-signature update separation, resource cost, and conditional latching.
Exact non-equality on a constructed real-valued witness is not a uniform error margin, and
table lookup is not recurrent tanh-tracker realizability.
Theorem set A: finite-state ceiling and 𝑆5 tracker
What to take from set A: at fixed width and precision the Emender is a finite-state machine,
and the 𝑆5 word problem it is tested on is a concrete, fully specified finite-state target.
• Finite-state ceiling at fixed precision. At fixed width and precision, every
online recognizer of this kind has only finitely many distinct states
(S5Witness.fixed_precision_state_space_finite). This bounds its online memory to
finitely many states; it does not prove strict containment below NC1
.
• 𝑆5 word problem. The target is pinned down exactly in Lean. The group 𝑆5 has 120
elements (S5Witness.s5_state_count) and is non-solvable (S5Witness.s5_not_solvable);
the prefix-product tracker that reads a word of transpositions and reports the running
product is a 120-state recognizer (S5Tracker.recognizer_state_count) whose state
composes correctly as tokens are appended (S5Tracker.run_append). A bridge lemma
confirms the Lean tracker agrees with the Python evaluation harness on every input

𝑆5: the tracker’s run map is surjective (S5Inseparability.run_surjective, via Mathlib’s
Equiv.Perm.mclosure_swap_castSucc_succ).

• Capacity lower bound (recognizer floor). Any finite-state recognizer with a
state decoder that reproduces the tracker on every input word needs
at least 120 states (S5Inseparability.capacity_lower_bound_120); equivalently,
no recognizer with fewer than 120 states can realize it under any
decoder (S5Inseparability.small_recognizer_cannot_realize_s5). The bound has

Theorem set B: generator-table lookup, not a recurrent tracker
The exact statement of NDMRealizesS5.emender_realizes_s5_tracker supplies 𝑑 = 12, four
orthonormal keys and one-hot values, queries, a decoder, and 𝜆 = 1. Reading a preloaded table
retrieves the current generator; decoding it and externally multiplying a supplied 𝑆5 state 𝑠
reproduces the transition 𝑠 ⋅ 𝑔. The linear readout retrieves a generator value, not the running
product. The supporting OnlineMemory.linearDeltaWrite_overwrites_one_preserves_others is
an exact pre-tanh overwrite statement. Neither theorem states that iterating the nonlinear cell
from zero tracks every input word.
Open item F1 — recurrent tanh-tracker realization unavailable. The loaded generator memory
does not carry the running group product; external composition supplies the missing state
computation. Closing F1 requires a configuration of the actual nonlinear update, an explicit
initial state and decoder, and an all-words trajectory theorem. This is not a missing proof of
the already-proved lookup identity, but a stronger, currently unproved architecture claim.
Theorem set C: update-family separation
What to take from set C: in a single step the delta-correcting write does something no fixed-
weight raw-write update of the matched shape can reproduce on the abstract witness domain.
The Emender’s delta-correcting write and M²RNN’s raw outer-product write are provably
distinct as update families over unrestricted real-valued keys, values, and states. The mixed key
is (1, 1) (not unit-normalized), and the prescribed initial state is nonzero. These witnesses are
not production zero-state trajectories with normalized keys and finite-logit sigmoid gates, nor
full-network separation under arbitrary reparameterization.
• Mechanism separation. The delta write exactly overwrites the
addressed slot while leaving every orthogonal query untouched
(OnlineMemory.linearDeltaWrite_overwrites_one_preserves_others), whereas the raw
outer-product write cannot satisfy that uniform one-step overwrite specification
(OnlineMemory.rawOuterWrite_not_uniformOneStepOverwrite).
33
• One-step resource separation. For every 𝐾 ≥ 2 and 𝑉 ≥ 1, no fixed-
weight M²RNN parameterization with row, column, or cell forget gates can
match the Emender’s mixed-key delta correction in a single recurrent step
(RecurrentResourceFormalism.emender_m2rnn_one_step_resource_separation_embeds).
The result is sharp: it covers every external-forget shape the M²RNN signature allows.
• Positive embedding. The separation is about resources, not raw capability.
Given the extra read-then-delta resource, M²RNN can embed one Emender step
(M2RNNComparison.m2rnn_read_then_delta_embeds_e88_delta_update); the previous bullet
says that without that extra resource it cannot.
Theorem set C′: multi-step (k-step) separation
What to take from set C′: the one-step gap does not average out under composition — it persists
at every length on a constructed input.
The one-step separation persists as exact non-equality at each finite length, not as a length-
independent accuracy margin.
For every 𝑘 ≥ 1, there is an explicit 𝑘-token input sequence such that the 𝑘-step trajectory
of any fixed-weight raw-write resource (in the row/column/cell external-forget class)
diverges from the Emender’s 𝑘-step trajectory by a nonzero entry for each finite 𝑘 (Lean:
emender_m2rnn_k_step_separation).
The witness alphabet is the 2-dimensional construction from set C followed by zero-
input filler tokens (kStepWitnessInputs k = (mixedKey, 0) :: zeroSteps (k - 1)). It
starts from the prescribed nonzero lowerLeftState, with unnormalized key (1, 1) and zero-
key fillers. Like set C, set C′ is over an unrestricted abstract real-valued signature, not
the production normalized-key, zero-initial-state domain. The proof inducts on the tail,
using a lemma that keeps entry (0, 0) pinned at zero on the raw-write side for every
𝑘 (FixedRightRawExternalForget2_preserves_zero_row), while the Emender side reduces to
the 𝑘-fold composition of tanh at −1, which is nonzero because tanh is injective. Its
magnitude need not stay bounded away from zero as 𝑘 grows; the statement uses exact real
arithmetic. A second form of the theorem packages the same statement existentially over 𝑘
-step input sequences (emender_m2rnn_k_step_separation_exists), and the two-step case is
exposed separately as the inductive base (emender_m2rnn_two_step_separation). All three live

9 Related Work
Ancestry: the fast-weight line on delta correction
The Emender update belongs to a long line that treats memory as a structure to correct rather
than to refill. The earliest formulation is Widrow and Hoff’s least-mean-square rule [41], which
writes the error between target and current output back into the weight vector of an adaptive
linear element. Schmidhuber recast the same idea in a recurrent setting under the name fast-
weight programmers [42]: a slow controller emits keys and values that update a fast-weight
matrix online, with the update being a correction against what the matrix currently predicts
at the addressed slot. Schlag, Irie, and Schmidhuber made the bridge to modern attention
explicit [43], showing that linear-transformer kernels with cumulative outer-product updates
are fast-weight programmers with a raw additive write, and proposing the delta-rule write as a
stability and capacity fix. DeltaNet [6] carried the delta rule into a parallelizable linear-recurrent
language model, demonstrating the rule at billion-parameter scale in the linear-state regime.
The Emender extends this line with three properties the linear instantiations forfeit: a nonlinear
matrix state, the saturation latching of Section 3 ingredient (c), which motivates persistent
binding under the conditional slot-level hold result (𝜆 ≥ 1 plus a write budget), not an achieved
production-default property; and width-axis parallelism via multi-programming that recovers
throughput without time-axis linearization.
Linear-state recurrent language models
The dominant comparison cohort is the family of linear-state billion-parameter recurrent LMs
surveyed in Section 2: Mamba [1] and Mamba2 [2] (selective SSMs); RetNet [44] (decayed
linear attention); GLA [4] (gated linear attention); DeltaNet [6] (delta rule, linear in 𝑆); Gated
DeltaNet [7] (gated delta rule); RWKV-4/5/6/7 [5], [45] (linear-state WKV / generalized delta
rule with linear state); HGRN2 [46] (gated linear RNN with state expansion); MinGRU/
MinLSTM [23] (input-only gates, linear scan); mLSTM/xLSTM-7B [47] (covariance update);
Griffin/RecurrentGemma [14] (RG-LRU, often hybridized with local attention). Circuit-
containment results for restricted SSM families require their stated transition and arithmetic
assumptions [8]; affinity alone is not a universal complexity classification. The empirical panel
in Section 6 directly tests Gated DeltaNet as the matched representative of this cohort and
observes 𝑆5 collapse at length.
In the broader SSM/sequence-model lineage, S5 [48] introduces simplified state-space layers with
a multi-input multi-output linear state-space recurrence. It is distinct from the permutation
group 𝑆5 used as a probe here; the cited S5 results concern sequence models, not billion-
parameter recurrent LMs.
A newer linear-state result, Gated DeltaNet-2 [16], decouples channel-wise erase and write gates
and is reported by its authors to outperform GDN. It extends retrieval and state-tracking
39
behavior inside the linear-state envelope, and it is the linear-recurrent baseline of this paper’s
1.3 B pairing: the GDN2-MLP arm of Section 5 is the official-style Gated DeltaNet-2 mixer
(external source commit 95709fc) with the same SwiGLU MLP as E97, selected by the same
CMA-ES protocol. The E97 split gates of Section 3 are the Emender’s corresponding edit — key-
axis erase/read plus value-axis write — inside a nonlinear state map, so the two architectures
are adjacent by design (Section 8, set G) rather than by coincidence. Its decoupled gates are
a natural next ablation for the multi-programmed substrate, being the kind of bounded per-
step-body variant that does not require a new time-parallel scan.
Pure-nonlinear-recurrent peers and adjacent nonlinear-state work
M²RNN [11] (Mishra, Tan, Stoica, Gonzalez, Dao 2026) introduces nonlinear matrix-state
recurrence with a raw-write update 𝑍 = tanh(𝐻𝑊 + 𝑘𝑣𝑇
). It shares the central systems premise
of this work — that a nonlinear recurrence is serial in time and must take its parallelism from
the batch and head axes — and demonstrates that the update trains at 410 M dense and 7 B
MoE scale on Nemotron-CC-v2. Its headline demonstrations and promoted deployment recipe,
however, use the nonlinear-matrix-recurrent layer sparingly inside hybrid stacks (interleaved
with attention layers), and its paper-default pure shape diverged as a pure recurrent backbone

xLSTM-1.3B [26] is a 7:1 mixture of mLSTM (linear) and sLSTM (nonlinear) blocks; with

10 Limitations
Formal gaps: G1–G3 and F1
G1 — no per-token circuit-size or depth lower bound.
S5GDNBridge.gdn_s5_realization_requires_per_token_action routes a hypothesized realizer’s
affine group actions through token-dependent parameters, but a four-symbol schedule may be
a shallow lookup. This matters because routing cannot establish that a GDN stack must pay
extra network depth or size. Closing it requires first resolving constrained realizability (G3),
then an explicit resource lower bound for the parameter-generating network.
G2 — state-dependent gates are unmodeled.
CompositionalDepthDuality.gdn_state_channel_depth_bounded_in_time and the bridge
condition on a state-independent schedule, not gates that read the carried state. This matters
because dynamic routing can introduce nonlinearity into that path and invalidate the affine-

and cost statements for that class.
G3 — constrained affine/reflection realizability is open. Whether the split-gated decay-minus-

the group with shallow token functions, while an obstruction would be algebraic rather than
a temporal-depth theorem. Closing it requires either a concrete representation satisfying the
production channel constraints (including allowed decay and gate ranges), or a non-realizability
proof for precisely those constraints. The Lean decay parameter is an arbitrary matrix, so the
formal family must not be silently identified with scalar-decay production restrictions.

Single-seed 1.3 B evidence and protocol boundaries
Both language-modeling arms and the current 1.3 B expressivity suite have one seed; the
multi-seed 8 M and grok-scale evidence is not replication of these trajectories. This matters
because seed variance and fine-tuning choices can change a terminal gap or its apparent length

intervals, common realized-budget controls, and fixed evaluation grids. The current modular-
quadratic comparison shares a cap and plateau rule but realizes 2,500 versus 2,200 steps;
intermediate GDN2 accuracy exceeds its terminal value, and its long-length plateau is not
chance. The 𝑆5 prediction fails on the pair, and GDN2′s attempted length-512 curriculum is
OOM-infeasible after one optimizer step. These facts matter because protocol and hardware
limits are alternative explanations; feasible matched curricula and fixed-step controls would test
them without rewriting the recorded negative. Large state-count headroom supports, but does
not prove, a learning-failure rather than constrained-capacity explanation.
Evaluation and winner-series evidence
The 150 B pair has no held-out tokenizer-invariant bpb or downstream panel; estimated 0.93
bpb and the matched-loss null use training logs, not a held-out evaluation or an equivalence test.
This matters because generalization and useful capability need not follow training-loss parity.
Closing it requires running the released evaluation pipeline on both immutable checkpoints and
reporting sample uncertainty without substituting predecessor results.
U3.1 — carried-in winner first-generation fitness unavailable. The retained CMA snapshots do
not supply the final E97-MLP/GDN2-MLP winner searches’ generation-zero fitness or spread;
E97′s normalized candidate/generation totals are also absent (Section 5). This matters because
their within-search improvement cannot be computed or attributed to tuning from the retained
rows. Closing it requires the winner searches’ original generation logs and identity mapping, not
imputation from earlier sweeps. Equal per-candidate rules are not proof of equal total search
effort; broader, matched-total-budget searches would test the residual optimization confound.
Untested application predictions
All six applications in Section 7 are structural identifications or analogies, and every E97/GDN
surrogate separation is OPEN. This matters because contraction, fixed support, alternative
encodings, finite precision, and the chosen error metric can erase an apparent nonlinear-depth
advantage. Closing it requires task-specific matched surrogates, long-horizon error and latency
measurements, and stability/safety checks for feedback control; energy or IoT deployment claims
additionally require hardware measurements. No such experiment is included here.

The 1.3 B pair is a viability demonstration, not a scaling law; the single-arm 513 B-token
Frontier continuation has no matched control. This matters because loss parity, memory costs,
and probe ordering may change at 3–7 B. Closing it requires a matched pair across scales under
one recipe. The raw-write comparator is only an 8 M and predecessor arm, and Emender defaults

A transformer was searched at short-budget CMA scale but has no matched 150 B-token arm;
training one under the paired recipe would close the long-run comparator gap.

Performance evidence is CUDA-only, and E88 occupancy does not establish E97 occupancy
or ROCm parity. This matters because source portability and shared kernels do not
guarantee backend correctness or throughput. Closing it requires paired ROCm correctness
and performance receipts at the reported geometry, with direct E97 occupancy measurements.
The bibliography audit separately flags entries whose publication metadata could not be
independently retrieved; correctness-first submission requires resolving those flags, not treating
citation resolution in Typst as literature verification.
11 Conclusion
The duality gives the paper’s structural distinction: E97 executes a nonlinear state update
at every step, whereas an input-only-scheduled GDN state fold remains affine in its initial
state (Section 8). Width-axis multi-programming makes the nonlinear route practical at 1.3
B; E97 trains through 150.8 B Pile tokens on one eight-GPU workstation with an estimated
training-log 0.93 bpb. Its parameter-matched, CMA-selected GDN2-MLP control occupies the
same training-loss band at matched tokens (Section 5); the current pair’s held-out evaluation
is still unavailable.
Direct probes distinguish capability from bulk loss. The iterated-map signature appears in
multi-seed small-scale controls and is replicated on the trained 1.3 B pair as a modular-quadratic
terminal-accuracy and length-robustness gap, not length-invariant tracking (Section 6). The
negative is equally load-bearing: “The 8M S5 separation does NOT replicate at 1.3B on this
pair.” The 1.3 B GDN2-MLP model is ahead on that group-state protocol. Real-valued update-
family separation and the 120-state recognizer floor do not predict what SGD must learn;
the generator-table lookup is not a recurrent tracker realization (F1), and affine-channel 𝑆5
realizability remains open (G3).
Repeated nonlinear maps in engineering and dynamics (Section 7) define a broader structural
scope, not six demonstrated deployments. Correctness of each formal statement, reproducible
artifact bindings, and disclosed negative transfers are the contribution’s basis for TMLR, rather
than priority, universal nonlinear superiority, or an unproved circuit-class separation. The next
tests are multi-seed common-budget probes, held-out pair evaluation, and the constrained
realizability questions of Section 10. This identified Arkheion preprint build does not establish
TMLR template or anonymization compliance; journal-specific submission packaging remains
a separate step.

12 Testable Predictions
The results above suggest concrete predictions, most of them testable at the next scale step or
under modest additional training. Each is stated as a claim with its scope and the observation
that would falsify it, so that later training rounds can settle them.
• Width-axis multi-programming scales beyond 1.3 B without throughput collapse. The multi-
programmed substrate harvests throughput from many small bounded heads rather than
from time-axis linearization; the per-head register/SRAM working set is independent of
total parameter count, so the recipe is expected to survive scale-up to the 3–7 B band.
Falsified if per-token throughput degrades faster than the parameter count grows, beyond
the kernel-arithmetic baseline.
• The Emender’s state-tracking advantage persists at scaled training budgets. The Section 6
𝑆5 and 𝑆3 gaps are at the 8 M parameter-matched probe scale under matched no-tuning,
and the iterated-map separation is at grok scale. At longer training-token budgets at 1.3
B and beyond, the within-PNR ordering and the Emender-vs-GDN2 gap on state-tracking
probes should hold qualitatively — the completed expressivity suite of Section 6 is the
first direct test, on the trained 150 B pair. The predicted 𝑆5 ordering already fails on
this pair under the tested protocols; the modular-quadratic result supports only a task-
dependent version. Further scale/budget persistence is falsified if that map gap closes
under a common optimization protocol.
44
• Emender fine-tuned on reasoning benchmarks separates from GDN2-MLP on tasks
requiring multi-step state-tracking. The Section 6 QA panels at the predecessor cohort’s
training budget sit below the threshold where reasoning benchmarks differentiate. After
targeted fine-tuning on a reasoning suite (BIG-Bench Hard, ReCLor, FOLIO, multi-step
object tracking), the Emender is expected to separate from the linear baseline specifically
on items where multi-step prefix tracking is load-bearing (object tracking, formal fallacies,
multi-step entailment). Falsified if E97 and GDN2-MLP remain within standard error on
state-tracking-shaped subsets after matched fine-tuning.
• PNR update-rule variants are per-step-body edits under the multi-programmed kernel. The
fused forward/backward Triton kernel (Section 4) is parameterized by the update map
and the gating. A different bounded PNR update rule at the same matrix-state signature
should be expressible by editing the per-step body, without satisfying an associative-scan
or chunkwise-WY compatibility constraint and with the multi-programmed time loop
unchanged. Falsified if a representative variant (e.g., decoupled erase/write gates in the
style of Gated DeltaNet-2 [16], or a higher-order delta with two-step memory) requires
kernel rewrites beyond the per-step body.
• Emender combined with hybrid attention should outperform either alone. The Section 6
hybrid-degradation result (Emender/GDN AABB underperforms either pure family on
state tracking at 8 M) is read here as a property of the Emender/GDN pairing specifically.
The prediction is that an Emender-with-attention hybrid (Emender layers interleaved
with attention layers, in the style of M²RNN’s hybrid configuration) outperforms
both pure Emender and pure attention on combined state-tracking and long-range
mixing benchmarks at matched parameter and token budgets. Falsified if the hybrid
underperforms either constituent at matched budget on the combined benchmark.
• A broadened CMA-ES sweep places the head-count interior optimum above the predecessor’s
𝐻 = 370. The predecessor-cohort searches (Section APPENDIX B) arrived at 𝐻 = 370
for E88 after successive bound repositionings against persistent upward pressure from the
optimizer. The conjecture is that the same fairness search under broader bounds, subject
only to the kernel-occupancy ceiling of the multi-programmed substrate, returns an interior
optimum at or above that band on the Emender at 1.3 B (E97′s own search selected 𝐻 =
216 within its configured bounds); 𝐻 ∼ 1000 is the next bracket worth testing. Falsified if a
broadened CMA-ES sweep under matched per-token compute returns an interior optimum
at or below the current selection.
Some of these will falsify; the falsifications are the next round of evidence.
13 Future Work
The results above leave several directions open. The first few bear directly on the empirical
comparison of Section 5 and Section 6 the formal targets that follow are the open ends of the
Lean development in Section 8.
A stronger raw-write baseline
The within-class ordering reported here — the Emender ahead of the raw-write update M²RNN-
CMA at the 8 M probes — is conditional on the no-tuning probe protocol, and the raw-write
family is not a 1.3 B arm (Section 1). One contrast in the current evidence stays entangled
45
because of that: the gap could be a genuine difference in what the two update rules express, or
it could be the residue of a raw-write configuration that received less effective optimization. The
way to separate the two is a stronger raw-write baseline — a matched state-tracking search on
the raw-write family at the 8 M probe shape, and a 1.3 B raw-write arm once its kernels run on
this stack — which distinguishes the conditional reading (“under the effort used here”) from the
family-level reading (“under any matched optimization”). It is the sharpest single experiment
for fixing where the Emender-versus-raw-write gap actually sits, and Section 10 names it as the
load-bearing follow-up.
Scale beyond 1.3 B
The matched-token loss parity is measured at the 1.3 B class on The Pile, at a single 30-
GPU-day training extent per arm. This is the named scale limitation of Section 10: whether
the parity holds at larger parameter counts, and whether the state-tracking separation grows
or vanishes with scale, is open. The load-bearing experiment is a matched 3–7 B E97/GDN2-
MLP pair under the same recipe; the single-arm E97 513 B-token Frontier continuation that
exists cannot answer a paired question, and a corpus move (e.g. to a license-clean stream) would
need its own matched pair. Section 12 states the throughput-scaling form of this question as a
falsifiable prediction.
Additional seeds and architecture-internal revalidation
Each 1.3 B run is a multi-week trajectory per architecture, so additional seeds at this band are
a multi-week investment apiece; the 1.3 B comparison is single-seed per architecture for that
reason, and a seed-averaged repeat is open. Several architecture-internal choices in the Emender
— the output gate, the state non-linearity (tanh versus linear), and the decay parameterization
(simple sigmoid versus Mamba2-style log-space) — tie on loss at small scale; revalidating each
at 1.3 B is open.
A capacity bound on the 𝑆5 generators themselves
The 𝑘-step separation proved in Section 8 (emender_m2rnn_k_step_separation, set C′) shows
that for every finite 𝑘 the gap between the delta-correcting and raw-write updates persists, but
it runs on a constructed two-dimensional witness alphabet rather than on the 𝑆5 generators. The
natural next-paper target is a capacity bound stated directly on the 𝑆5 generator alphabet: for
any fixed-weight raw-write recurrence at state dimension 𝑑, an explicit length 𝑇(𝑑) past which
tracking the 𝑆5 cosets becomes unreachable, while the Emender at the same 𝑑 still tracks them.
That bound needs a bounded-precision raw-write class and Merrill-style state-counting lower
bounds not yet available in Mathlib. The Section 6 length-extrapolation curves on parity, finite-
state tracking, and the modular counter show positive but non-monotone gaps. For parity the
gap peaks near 0.359 at 𝑇 = 320, then narrows to about 0.337 at 𝑇 = 500. These are retained-
advantage observations, not evidence of the proposed 𝑆5 capacity bound or of a uniformly
widening gap.
A partial order on PNR update rules
The ordering established here, delta-correcting above raw-write, is a noncontainment witness
between two matched-signature update families, not a proved strict set-inclusion order on the
PNR class (emender_m2rnn_one_step_resource_separation_embeds, set C in Section 8). Whether
46
that relation extends to a partial order over the broader PNR space — whether antisymmetry
and transitivity hold across the full space, under an explicit common resource class — is open.
The horizon is the maximal element: which PNR update rule carries the highest one-step
expressive power for its compute class. The RecurrentResourceFormalism, the Lean signature
in which the Section 8 separations are stated, is the tool for climbing that order. More flexible
bounded update rules are the natural rungs, among them split erase/write variants in the style
of Gated DeltaNet-2 [16]; placing them in the order is open work.
Lifting the latching theorems to the whole layer
The Section 8 latching set establishes the slot-level half of the Emender primitive: three
theorems covering saturation insensitivity, default hold under a bounded delta, and release
on a sufficient counter-delta. Two formal targets sit on top of it. The first is a formalism
refinement — an architecture-level lift that promotes the slot-wise latching theorems to the
RecurrentResourceFormalism.MemorySemantics.latchAttractor label on the Emender signature
in the RecurrentResourceFormalism, recasting slot-level behavior as a statement about the
matrix-state attractor structure of the whole layer. The second is a Lean-targeted theorem that
connects Section 8 to the Section 6 empirics: a basin-survival bridge that takes an 𝑆5 coset in a
future recurrent realization (first closing F1) and shows it survives an adversary whose per-slot
perturbation budget stays below the Section 8 release threshold, lifting the slot-level guarantees
to a coset-level guarantee on the state-tracking task.

Per-architecture CMA-ES protocol
All three 1.3 B-class architectures (Emender, M²RNN-CMA, Gated DeltaNet) received
independent CMA-ES [17] hyperparameter and shape search over the same six knobs: width,
depth, head count, state width, output gating, learning rate. Each search ran under matched
candidate budget: population 16, fixed wall-clock per candidate, identical fitness rule of mean
training nats over a fixed late-training window. When CMA-ES drifted onto a configured-
range edge, the range was repositioned and search continued, applied identically across the
three architectures. The per-family winner shapes in the table above carry into the Section 6
expressivity probes and Section 8 formal analysis unchanged. The concurrent M²RNN paper
(Mishra et al. [11], section 5.2) holds width, depth, optimizer, learning rate, weight decay and
gradient clipping uniform across compared architectures and varies only the sequence-mixing
block, a fair-by-uniformity protocol that does not give each architecture its own best-tuning.
The per-architecture search was adopted to avoid the undisclosed HPO budgets common in
nearby work and the contradictory within-class results that follow from them. It also functioned
as a measurement. On the Emender, CMA-ES repeatedly pushed head count up against
whatever ceiling the bounds set, and each range repositioning revealed the same pressure on
the next iteration. The final 𝐻 = 370 is the interior optimum reached after the bounds were
placed far enough out that CMA-ES stopped against open ground. A search configured for
cross-architecture fairness thus placed its optimum at high head count without being tuned
toward it: throughput comes from many small, independently addressed recurrent programs.

Narrow observed loss bands under matched compute
The narrow observed bands occur under matched compute for each update rule. The three
rules — delta-correcting (E88), raw-write (M²RNN-CMA), and linear (GDN) — each received
the same per-architecture CMA-ES hyperparameter-and-shape search budget and were then
trained to matched FLOPs, and they converge to within ≈ 0.005–0.006 bpb of one another on
both axes: held-out 0.966 / 0.966 / 0.961 and train-loss 0.973 / 0.973 / 0.979. Equal tuning effort
and equal compute land three structurally different recurrences in the same narrow loss band.
After each family is tuned to its own best operating point, the converged losses occupy a narrow
observed band; this does not establish equal loss across update rules. Where they come apart
is the Section 6 state-tracking probes, not the reported bpb bands, and so far that separation
shows only on synthetic algebraic state-tracking (𝑆5/parity/FSM, Section 6) — tasks that no
model in the cohort solves. The non-synthetic signals — bulk held-out loss, the QA panel, and
the reasoning panel (Section 5) — occupy narrow observed bands, without a tested equivalence
claim.
The loss figures of this appendix are single-seed per architecture. The five held-out Pile
slices (Section APPENDIX C) show slice-dependent ordering of the lowest-bpb model and
narrow observed bands of between-model means; their cross-slice standard deviations do
not measure uncertainty of a paired difference. The Common-Pile contamination control
(Section APPENDIX C) also shows descriptive closeness on a second distribution. These
observations do not establish an architecture-agnostic compute-optimal loss at this matched
FLOP budget. The state-tracking results that carry the paper do not rest on these single-seed
loss runs: they rest on the 3-seed 8 M expressivity probes (Section 6) and the 1.3 B length-
generalization separation (below), where the architectures genuinely come apart.

Trained-length competence vs length-extrapolation plateau. Two facts must be kept apart. First,
at the trained length (𝑇 = 64) the delta model fits the held-out 𝑆5 prefixes substantially better:
E88 reaches 0.921, near-solving a non-solvable task, whereas this linear-recurrent GDN run

this GDN run under-fits them; this is not an acquisition impossibility. Second, under length-
extrapolation (out to 𝑇 = 1024, 16× the trained length) all three degrade; E88 degrades the
slowest and stays strictly ahead at every length, but it plateaus below ceiling (0.143 at 𝑇 = 512,
0.076 at 𝑇 = 1024). This is a measured length-generalization plateau, not a proved capacity
limit or expressive impossibility: the 𝑇 = 512 curve is flat from ≈ 12, 000 steps at 2× M²RNN’s
budget under constant LR (see The delta update is also length-bounded below). The accurate
statement is therefore neither “E88 length-generalizes on 𝑆5” nor “E88 fails 𝑆5”: the delta
update learns 𝑆5 at the trained length and leads at every length, with its length-extrapolation
plateauing below ceiling at this width.

stronger on parity and 𝑆3. These are protocol-specific observations, not proof of a non-solvable
computation barrier. The GDN2-MLP reversal in Section 6 and open constrained realizability
question G3 prevent a families-wide interpretation.

studying length generalization; it does not isolate capacity from optimization. A late-rate
extrapolation suggesting only about 0.03 further gain is not a measured doubled-budget result.
The supported statement is that neither tested nonlinear arm reaches ceiling at extrapolated
length, while E88 remains ahead under the recorded budgets.
Generalization beyond seen sequences. Evaluation words are held out and test-disjoint; the
generator alphabet gives exponentially many possible words. Above-chance accuracy out to 𝑇 =
1024 supports learning transferable structure rather than memorizing the enumerated training

examples. It does not establish exact algorithm recovery or exclude finite-context heuristics. The

Raw-write’s shortfall is partly trainability. M²RNN’s 𝑆5 (and 𝑆3) accuracy was still slowly rising

QA and reasoning panel at 1.3 B: parity-rate evidence
For capability beyond loss numbers we evaluate the three 1.3 B-class models on a 300-item
multi-choice continuation harness sampled from ARC-C/E [50], HellaSwag [51], SciQ [52],
OpenBookQA [53], and BoolQ [54]. At the current training budget, E88 reaches 0.367 (random
0.29), GDN 0.380, and M²RNN-CMA 0.367; all three sit within one standard error of one
another (SE ≈ 6 pp at 50 items per task). On a separate reasoning panel (BIG-Bench Hard
[55], ReCLor [56], FOLIO [57]), all three families collapse on multi-step object tracking (the
seven-object shuffled-objects tracking task, 0.10–0.13, near-random) and on FOLIO/ReCLor

class-level claim at this training budget. The state-tracking-specific reasoning separation, if it
exists, will emerge at longer training horizons; Section 12 states the prediction explicitly.
APPENDIX C Held-out bits-per-byte measurement
(predecessor cohort)
This appendix is the evidentiary backbone of the held-out descriptive closeness reported
in Section APPENDIX B: it documents exactly how the held-out bits-per-byte figures are
measured and places them in a landscape of reference models and classical compressors. Every
number is measured by the eval harness described below; none is re-fitted. The measurement
56
code, slice manifests, and re-exported checkpoints are public (see the availability statement
in Section 11).
Tokenizer-invariant held-out bpb
So that models with different tokenizers are comparable on identical bytes, held-out bpb is
computed from each model’s real summed negative log-likelihood over the slice and the slice’s
real UTF-8 byte count:
bpb = (total NLLnats/ ln 2)/ total UTF-8 bytes.
Each model uses its own tokenizer, and the denominator is the shared byte count, so bytes/
token is a measured per-tokenizer quantity (4.00 for the GPT-NeoX BPE, 3.82 for our p50k_base
runs on the canonical slice) — not the fixed bytes/token = 3.92 constant used for the train-
loss curve in Section 5. The held-out and train-loss figures are therefore distinct objects: they
differ in normalization as well as in the data scored. Every token is scored once under a sliding
window (context 2048, stride 1024) with up to 2047 tokens of left context, the standard fixed-
length-model perplexity recipe; out-of-distribution anchors GPT-2 XL and OPT-1.3B run at
context 1024 (GPT-2 XL position limit). Pipeline: scripts/measure_pile_bpb.py (open models)
and scripts/measure_pile_bpb_elman.py (our models, in-harness forward).
Held-out slice provenance
The canonical held-out slice is a whole-line 10 MB region (9,999,511 bytes, sha256 3e4241a9…)
taken at byte offset 1012
(≈76.5% into the 1.31 TB Pile master file) — a deep offset the 1.3
B runs’ sub-one-epoch stream from the file start is least likely to have consumed. The five-
slice robustness check of Section 5 adds four further independent, non-overlapping, sha-verified
slices at offset fractions 0.137 / 0.341 / 0.523 / 0.911, each ≈10 MB and ≈2.5 M scored tokens.
Identical bytes feed every model, so the byte denominator is shared and bpb is comparable
across tokenizers. A block-loss sanity gate (mean nats/token on the first 2048-token block must
lie in [1.5, 4.0], model train loss ≈2.6) ran before any of our models’ bpb was reported; all gated
runs passed. The sha-verified slice manifests are released with the measurement code (see the
Section 11 availability statement).
Second distribution: Common Pile (contamination control)
Because the open Pile-trained references (Pythia, GPT-Neo) saw the Pile in training, their
Pile numbers are effectively in-distribution. As an independent check we re-measure through
the identical pipeline on a held-out slice of Common Pile (v0.1 main-mix; 9,999,606 bytes,
document-aligned on the 0x1E record separator, at a random deep offset 65.5% into its 1 TB
corpus), a permissively-licensed corpus none of these models trained on. The Pile-trained models
score within ≤ 0.003 bpb of their Pile numbers there (Pythia-1.4B +0.0028, GPT-Neo-1.3B
+0.0002, Pythia-1B +0.0003), so the Pile result is not contamination-inflated. Our three models
on the same Common-Pile slice measure Emender 0.981, GDN 0.963, M²RNN-CMA 0.973 —
within ≈0.02 bpb of their Pile figures, with ordering GDN < M²RNN-CMA < Emender (again
not the train-loss ordering), consistent with a small distribution shift rather than a blow-up.
Best classical coder on Common Pile is xz −9 at 2.061 bpb.
