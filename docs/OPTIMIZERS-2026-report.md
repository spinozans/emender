## 1. Recommendation by regime

1. **8M repeated-map instrument:** run the registered Muon / AdamW+cosine / SF-constant comparison first. AdamW+cosine is the positive control; SF-constant is the pathology control. Hybrid Muon is the strongest new hypothesis, not an observed winner. “8M” is the historical instrument label: the exact X9 E97 configuration has **9,900,800 trainable parameters** [I1].
2. **Production 512-step fine-tunes:** finish the already registered W3 comparison unchanged. Favor a tuned ordinary AdamW schedule as the first alternative to incumbent SF; add a separate Muon arm only after a clean Stage 1 and full-size numerical/serialization preflight. **Muon at our 512-step low-LR polish horizon is unestablished**, and Moonlight supplies an affirmative pretrain/SFT-mismatch warning [P1 §3.5]. Do not retrofit a third arm into W3 or read its partial scores to choose settings.
3. **Long pretraining / 8B handoff under DiLoCo:** hybrid Muon with Moonshot's decay and update-RMS recipe is the leading experimental candidate. MuLoCo supplies unusually direct inner-optimizer evidence [P3], but its outer Nesterov optimizer is not our stateless-average default. Evaluate a **Muon-inner adaptation** first; reserve the name “MuLoCo replication” for a separately approved matching outer-loop experiment. No 2x speedup, resilient convergence, or 8B transfer is promised.

Keep SF repairs and SOAP as second-line, regime-specific experiments; retain AdEMAMix/MARS as credible inexpensive-to-medium-cost challengers if Muon fails or the horizon favors them. Do not substitute a catalog of new optimizers for matched tuning and measured wall time.

## 4. Muon-family receipts and the equal-tuning correction

### What is genuinely compelling

- **Moonlight [P1], abstract and §§2.2–3.3:** weight decay and shape-adjusted update RMS enabled scaling; approximately **2x compute efficiency** is the authors' compute-optimal scaling-law result, not a universal wall-time claim. Moonlight is **3B activated / 16B total MoE**, trained on **5.7T tokens**, not two separately trained dense models. Their recipe scales an orthogonalized update `O` for an `m × n` matrix by `0.2 * sqrt(max(m,n))`, uses decoupled decay, five NS iterations and momentum .95. They keep AdamW for embeddings, LM head and norms, even when embeddings/head are two-dimensional.
- **Kimi K2 [P2], abstract, Algorithm 1 and §2.5:** **1T total / 32B activated** MoE; authors report **15.5T tokens with zero loss spikes** using MuonClip. The recipe is decay **.1**, update-RMS matching, QK-Clip **tau=100**; WSD has **500-step warmup**, LR **2e-4** over the initial **10T** tokens, then cosine to **2e-5** over **5.5T**. This is important stability/deployment evidence, not “Muon alone guarantees no spikes,” and **WSD is not schedule-free**. E97 lacks K2's MLA softmax-logit geometry; tau=100 cannot simply be copied.
- **MuLoCo [P3], abstract and §§4/6:** studies 150M, 416M, 914M, 1.76B, 3.1B, plus a 15B extrapolation experiment. Reports more directionally aligned pseudogradients, absolute advantage over AdamW-inner DiLoCo, and a relative-to-own-DP-baseline advantage for **more than two workers**. Quantization, streaming and long local intervals are studied. §4 uses **outer SGD with Nesterov momentum**, not our default stateless mean. In paper notation workers = `K`, local steps = `H`; here local steps = `DILOCO_K=40`. This is direct algorithmic motivation for our architecture, **not** direct evidence for E97, nonuniform/changing participation, restart policy, asynchronous corrections, or our production outer optimizer.
- **MONA [P5]** adds an EMA of gradient differences, with MoE results spanning 1B–68B (largest trained on 1T tokens). This is a separate acceleration mechanism, not proof that another momentum switch will solve our instrument. **NorMuon:** retain as a credible family lead, but the brief's exact Microsoft **+21.74%** number remains supervisor-supplied [S-N].

### Why this is not “Muon always wins”

1. **Equal tuning changes the headline.** [P4] studies ten optimizers, 0.1B–1.2B, 1–8x Chinchilla ratios: the fastest matrix-based methods' advantage shrinks from about **1.4x to 1.1x**, with ranking sensitive to scale, data/model ratio and endpoint. Do not turn that into an 8B extrapolation or compare an unannealed baseline with a tuned cooldown. The brief's ICLR-2026 venue label was not independently proceedings-verified.
2. **Tokens/FLOPs are not seconds.** [P14] uses a common 23-candidate development budget, matched seeds and small reference implementations. Its language models favor Muon at equal data but AdamW on time-normalized loss area; full Muon calls consume 56.1–69.6% of training time versus 6.1–9.1% for AdamW. Those are small-model/reference-kernel observations, **not** an optimized 8B ordering. They are especially relevant to our small instrument: time every arm.
3. **Horizon changes the problem.** [P12] actually compares **Muon, SOAP, ADANA, AdamW**, 51M–253M, overtraining factors 1–256x. It reports roughly constant Muon/SOAP token-efficiency advantages over most of that range, possible SOAP gains at the highest factors, and ADANA overtaking Muon at the high end. It does **not** establish “Kron wins heavy overtraining”; that broader brief statement is unverified. The defensible lesson is to tune memory, decay and schedule for the actual horizon.
4. **Other winners exist.** [P7], Fig. 1/§4.3, ranks **AdEMAMix and MARS** highest in its 720M Llama setup. It carefully tunes optimizers; this is not proof of perfectly equal search cost in every comparison. [P13] relates AdEMAMix/SF/accelerated SGD theoretically, with preliminary 150M experiments—not equivalence of behavior on E97.
5. **SFT is not absent from Muon literature, but our SFT regime is.** Moonlight §3.5 tests two-epoch Tulu SFT and public Qwen2.5-7B. It reports no significant Muon advantage when switching from AdamW pretraining, and comparable public-model SFT performance. MONA also reports SFT. Neither establishes low-LR **512-update E97 polish from an SF-trained basin**. This is a reason for caution, not a reason to assert all Muon fine-tuning is unstudied.
6. **SF research includes repairs and positive results.** [P6] explicitly says SF-AdamW “consistently underperforms well-tuned AdamW baselines” in its context and proposes SF-NorMuon; [P11] describes momentum/batch sensitivity **and** an improved variant; [P10] reports long-horizon ScheduleFree+ gains. None licenses declaring all SF-family methods broken.

Fair local comparison therefore requires **two ledgers**: fixed-token/update outcome and measured end-to-end seconds (including optimizer, evaluation, compilation amortization and checkpoint costs). An initial fixed-recipe screen is not an equal-tuned ranking. A later ranking must predeclare equal GPU-hour/search budgets, candidate ranges, development-only selection, seed allocation, validation endpoint and an untouched confirmatory read. Blindly copying AdamW's LR to unscaled Muon is unfair; RMS matching makes transfer interpretable, not guaranteed optimal. Charge auxiliary AdamW and all failed trials to Muon's budget too.

## 5. Concrete implementation cost for E97 (specification, not code changes)

Source anchors: [`train.py`](../train.py) (`build_training_optimizer`, `save_checkpoint`, `load_checkpoint`, SF point preparation and DiLoCo flags); [`E97 wrapper`](../ndm/models/e97.py); shared [`E88/E97 core`](../ndm/models/e88_fla_hybrid.py); [`hybrid ladder`](../ndm/models/hybrid_ladder.py); [`SF offload`](../ndm/schedulefree_offload.py). Current trainer choices are AdamW and SF, not a ready-made Muon switch.

**Parameter routing:** trainable E97 fast-weight **projection parameters** are 2D matrices (q/k/v, output and split-edit gate projections), making them eligible for matrix updates. The recurrent per-example **fast-weight state itself is not an optimizer parameter**. Use a reviewed semantic allowlist of hidden projection/MLP matrix weights for Muon; route embeddings, tied vocabulary head, norms, biases and scalar/vector knobs to AdamW. Do **not** use `ndim == 2` alone: e.g. core `value_residual` and some biases are 2D but are not hidden linear maps. Deduplicate tied embedding/head storage and bind one ownership assignment per unique trainable parameter; preserve registered decay exclusions and knob LR multipliers. Any eligible-set expansion must be named in registration.

**Minimal integration, medium cost:** choose one pinned Muon implementation; record momentum convention/Nesterov choice, NS coefficients/iterations, shape scaling, epsilon/zero-gradient handling, dtype and decay. Start with full local matrices and ordinary PyTorch matmuls; no custom recurrent-kernel rewrite is intrinsically required. Supply a single optimizer facade with explicit `param_groups`/`base_lr`, `zero_grad`, `step`, `state_dict` and `load_state_dict` covering both Muon and auxiliary AdamW. Step and schedule both exactly once per training update. Never invent SF `x/z` points for Muon; identify its ordinary live/saved point explicitly.

**Production integration, high cost:** deterministic group order/identity, typed optimizer family/recipe version, strict incompatible-resume rejection, accurate scheduler clocks, FP32 master/accumulator policy versus BF16 updates/stochastic rounding, finite checks, clipping-before-orthogonalization order, and true restart parity. W3's existing typed AdamW facade is a precedent, not proof of hybrid compatibility. SF CPU-offload is not a generic Muon offloader: Muon needs coherent matrix momentum/gradients; bucket streaming or sharding arbitrary matrix slices changes the algorithm. Estimate peak bytes including matrices, NS scratch, FP32 masters, AdamW moments and checkpoint staging before loading 8B. Fewer Muon moment buffers do not automatically mean lower peak memory.

**Triton/CUDA/ROCm:** optimizer matmuls occur after model backward; existing recurrent Triton kernels can initially remain unchanged. Many small matrices/NS iterations may dominate 8M wall time. Profile first, then batch/fuse only with reference parity. CUDA-only distributed-Muon or Triton code does not establish Frontier HIP/ROCm support; validate compiler, precision and GEMM behavior on the target GPU. Full-matrix requirements constrain ZeRO/TP/EP integration [P1 §2.3]; never silently orthogonalize flattened merge buckets. Singleton GPU islands avoid that particular sharding problem but not memory cost.

**DiLoCo:** communicate normal model displacement, including both parameter groups, in the existing layout; do not average optimizer buffers as though they were model weights. Pin whether Muon momentum and auxiliary AdamW moments are retained or reset after each merge and after a human-approved restart. First keep the selected production outer rule fixed; compare its trajectory against a small deterministic reference. Paper-style outer Nesterov is another experiment with another state schema. SF-specific async `x/z/interval` correction cannot be reused for Muon by renaming tensors; changing async semantics requires a separately reviewed derivation.

Required implementation tests before any task training: exhaustive unique grouping/tied aliases; square/tall/wide/zero-gradient updates against reference; update-RMS/decay isolation; auxiliary AdamW parity; scheduler endpoints; finite BF16/FP32 updates; save/reload next-update parity; wrong-family resume rejection; K-boundary merge/reference math; fresh-process restart; measured peak memory and optimizer time. This report adds no implementation or test code.
