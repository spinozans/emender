# S20: start the positional worker on its first unresolved read, not at every admission

**Qualified opt-in for synchronous sparse serving on the retained 150M byte host.** With [`ScopedRecords(..., defer_position=True)`](scoped_records.py), sparse DIRECT/ENTER made all the same predictions without starting a locator in any of its four waves. Mean first offer → final release fell **5.205 → 4.786 s**; offer → first p95 **4,681 → 4,297 ms**. The unrestricted sampled program really needed the locator in two waves out of four. Demand started it in those requests and nowhere else, returned exactly the same results, and **increased** the mean worst tick containing native work from **1,135 to 1,279 ms**. That 1.127× increase was within the registered ≤1.25× guard, not a tail-latency win.

**The deferred work is identified, not erased.** Each mode hashes the positional executable at admission and pins it and the index; it also verifies the whole branch grant, exact descriptor and opened text fd. On a real launch both modes pass the *same pinned files* into the native worker and verify its handshake and executable identity. No starts are triggered by retrieving a reader, `contents`, `following`, an empty frame, teardown, or an exact histogram certificate. An unresolved key cannot be silently treated as absent. A failed birth/request revokes the companion, not a signal to retry it. Unused grants have `pid`, `rc`, `birth`, `start_ns` and native peak **null**, not fabricated clean exits. The [tests](test_demand_position.py) include real F6 text/value access before birth, mixed certified/invalid/unresolved keys, stale and wrong-thread handles, late failure and post-birth death.
