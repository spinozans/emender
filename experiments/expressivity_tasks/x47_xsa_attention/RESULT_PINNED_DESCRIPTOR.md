# S16: keep the checked descriptor, start a fresh branch worker for every group

**Recommend descriptor reuse for the tested four-wave whole-P100 workload. Do not keep the m20 process between waves.** In paired fresh-process cells, mean time from before first verification through final close fell from **17.095 to 6.491 s (.380×)**. The worst m20 peak stayed at **261 MiB**. The change saved repeated mount verification; it didn't make the model, LF walk, or native row protocol faster.

Primary wall and summed-open ratios **.380/.178**, worst main/native peak ratios **.994/.9996**: all met the preregistered .85/.60/1.20/1.20 limits. `exact_descriptor_gate=true`, `descriptor_image_recommended=true`. **Use the within-S16 contrast.** Fresh verification here took much longer than in S15 (four opens averaged 1.208 s there vs 12.921 s here). The S16 inner fresh binary/descriptor checks took 11.366/14.447 s; the handshakes did not. We did not control background load/OS-cache state or isolate the variable within verification. Two cells per configuration do not establish a universal speed ratio. End-to-end subprocess wall averaged **24.65 vs 14.07 s**, not .380×.

**What is reused.** [`P100Image`](branch_image.py) pins the registered executable/index/text and two sidecars and verifies the binary SHA, descriptor directory/CRC, extents, sidecar SHAs and document boundaries. It defensively copies and compares **all 16,119,767 int64 starts (128,958,136 bytes)** once; a stale NumPy view cannot change another wave's offsets or shape. The image has a single PID/thread owner, finite wave limit, source/version/whole-scope checks and a one-use native launch ticket. The descriptor is not authority to open a worker. Each wave gets its own checked `W3BP0001` handshake (root = nodes = 21,707,038), private pipes, one revocable grant and empty 256-page LRU. Pins and worker liveness are checked on the read path, including cache hits; 585 image identity checks per four-wave panel. A poisoned wave can't hand off its worker. No previous m20 process, reply cache, live client, positional handle, neural value or memory register is reused by the next group. This is a trusted Linux owner, not a multi-tenant isolation proof or a hash of the full 65 GB index.

**Work matched, including S15.** Each cell made 1,024 predictions over four disjoint groups of eight TRAIN documents: predict at each of their first 32 observed bytes using the bound T4 retained bytewise packet. Waves 0/2 used the frozen trained T9 direct head; 1/3 freely sampled the P2 program. No gradient, shifted-target NLL or feedback of the argmax. All per-document complete byte-score/trust-or-joint/argmax/evidence hashes, dispatches and native row request/reply hashes matched S15 fresh-A. Per cell: **1,040 `R` frames, 2,866 rows, 8,844,008 wire bytes** incl. four handshakes; no `S` frames. Fifteen sampled archive occurrence keys all exceeded cap (three native, 80 ranks, zero LF steps). No claim about positive positional walk latency. All **16 m20** and **72 separate positional/live** workers exited cleanly, each m20 reaped before its successor; all expired-grant probes refused. Per-wave wall (fresh A / image A / image B / fresh B, s): wave 0 direct .830/.786/.757/.778; 1 sampled 1.391/1.300/1.349/1.251; 2 direct .598/.713/.628/.643; 3 sampled 1.233/1.242/1.349/1.302. Not a generated-byte benchmark or a claim about GPU decoding.
