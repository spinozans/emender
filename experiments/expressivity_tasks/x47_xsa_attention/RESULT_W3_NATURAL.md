# W3: the second-successor join did not beat direct retrieval on natural bytes

**Decision: keep the direct model; do not scale this join.** The W2 mechanism could be trained at useful exposure. Its older record and two archive children were available on 79.47% of development positions, but the fitted model assigned the join just **0.051% of mixture weight** on those positions. Removing the association between each edge and its child answer barely changed all-byte NLL. A separately trained direct mixture scored better. The registered development gate failed; W3 confirmation documents were selected by metadata but **not extracted or scored**.

## A real document-level test

We mounted the existing P100 rehearsal XSA over **100,000,000,000 bytes**, specifically its depth-8 boundary-aware m20 trie, and used the dense C14 Transformer already trained on 972,375,733 raw-byte targets. That frozen LM supplies causal hidden states and a normalized 256-byte prediction, using its original 128-byte reset blocks. Four new 95,726-parameter workbench heads trained independently from the same initialization and replayed the same inputs in the same order. No markers, supplied tags, synthetic codebooks, or selection by whether memory worked.

Each arm saw **4,194,304 unique shifted targets**, a contiguous 2,048-target window in each of **2,048 SHA-verified Pile documents**. One pass, 512 AdamW updates at four documents/update. Development: **262,144 targets in 128 other documents**, including every first byte predicted after a reset and all misses. The source windows start beyond byte 100.6 billion; the trained backbone's original source span ends before 59.2 billion. Selection used the existing registered split, length eligibility and a deterministic digest ordering; old panel hashes were excluded. No document or window crossing. This removes direct source-span inclusion, not the possibility of a duplicate passage elsewhere in the Pile. It is one seed in the population of documents long enough for this window.

**What the natural program does.** For the current suffix, direct sources include (1) the pretrained LM, (2) the exact next-byte distribution at the deepest available P100 suffix among 8/6/4/2/1/root, (3) causal, document-local 8/4/2 backoff, and (4) the first successor of an earlier matching suffix. For the additional join, find an older occurrence of a materialized 4-byte suffix, or back off to 2. Its *second* successor and the hidden state computed when that byte was observed supply the write. The writer must be at least 16 bytes old. Read the child distributions of the two most frequent outgoing byte edges at the locus, only if both are materialized with positive byte mass. A four-slot attention computation combines current state, recalled computation, and two bound edges. It can change the **current** prediction. This is a bounded pair proposal plus full-support direct prediction, not an exact two-edge characterization of the archive.

The write starts as the actual historical tag, the pair score as parent frequency × `(1 + 32 × child probability of that tag)`. Training adjusts the write, edge scores, and source trust. The schedule for seeking/writing/choosing the two edges is fixed; the experiment does **not** show learned hard navigation. Because that schedule is causal and weight-independent, previous writer positions and compact archive pages can be batched. The trainable value is computed in the graph at the old position and gathered later, not cached from a different checkpoint. Target-specific likelihood gathers are kept out of the head's inputs.

## All-byte development result (nats/byte, lower is better)

| Mode | NLL | Control − full, paired 95% document bootstrap |
|---|---:|---:|
| Frozen neural LM only | 1.409186 | +0.331404 [0.298087, 0.373595] |
| Trained direct mixture; no join vote | **1.077088** | **−0.000694 [−0.000837, −0.000554]** |
| Trained blind: pool child distributions across candidate edges | 1.077786 | +0.0000045 [−0.0000608, +0.0000670] |
| Fixed tag and pair rule, trained trust | 1.077786 | +0.0000046 [−0.0000007, +0.0000100] |
| Full trainable workbench join | 1.077782 | reference |
| Full weights, blind only at evaluation | 1.077800 | +0.0000182 [−0.0000185, +0.0000524] |
| Full weights, erase recalled tag **and computed hidden payload** | 1.080095 | +0.002314 [0.001716, 0.002868] |

The direct twin still has the direct historical copy, the old projected hidden state, current hidden and pooled child-summary inputs to source trust. It cannot vote for an edge through the join expert. The fixed twin can fit trust from the same workspace; the blind twin can still score candidate IDs and use their priors. These are **matched information/shape controls**, not a measured implementation that saves every child read. The large improvement over neural alone is from the combined system, **not attributable to the join**.

**Opportunity vs benefit.** On development, 211,738 positions had an eligible historical address, 208,334 had a usable child pair, and 122,985 of those pairs contained the actual next byte (59.03%). Mean age of an active read: 273.4 bytes. Even restricted to active positions, full scored 1.071462 vs direct 1.070668. Full beat direct on only 18/128 docs. All 65,536 initially zero writer weights changed in full, yet the final mean join gate on active positions was 0.000511. Erasing the entire recalled payload hurt more than pooling the children. This is evidence that the recalled state affected some predictions/trust, **not** that the edge-specific relation helped, nor a comparison to a freshly trained no-state control. The intervals measure variability across documents, not seeds. Required gain ≥.001 and lower paired bound >0 against each of direct/fixed/blind: **failed**.

## How it was made efficient and checked

- Native SXGC `5f02c11` adds a one-open `branch-of INDEX --workspace` interface over the existing m20. For a window it advances the available suffix nodes one observed byte at a time, reusing immutable node transitions. It then returns full, document-bounded byte/END histograms and child references for distinct requested nodes. The implementation never locates corpus positions or invents a count for an unmaterialized node. Node IDs are transient, usable only inside the bound mount; shards retain compact probabilities/operands, not portable SA coordinates. Held-out raw text is not written to Git.
- **19 CPU tests passed**, including W1/W2 regressions, every-prefix vs batched features with changed target, independent live counts, seam/hash rejection, analytic birth scores, dense-vs-target probability and gradient to the earlier writer. The frozen backbone also matched its standalone output, and changing future inputs left checked prefix probabilities/hidden states unchanged (GPU maxima 0). Four stock CLI queries checked the mount; a **separate 12-call stock replay** checked three real train positions, including native parent and both child rows. Max child probability error <2.1e−8 there, not an exhaustive P100 certification. An initial stock-audit import failed before any query; its error and correction are retained. No scoring rule or model changed.
- **63.0 s** total to select/hash, qualify and prepare train+dev CPU pages (~1.24 GB of local arrays). Shared frozen backbone once per batch, independent optimizers. **22.17 s** wall for the entire four-arm GPU training loop, including loading shards and saving checkpoints; synchronized shared backbone portion **0.79 s**. **0.67 s** to score all dev modes. These are batched teacher-forced measurements, **not serial per-byte decode costs**. For comparison W2's per-byte Python rounds were a mechanism witness, not the training path used here.
