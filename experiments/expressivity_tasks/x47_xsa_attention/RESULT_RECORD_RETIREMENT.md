# S18: closing a native worker need not release the table its readers reference

**Retire the three record readers with their grant in synchronous code.** In both ordinary six-wave processes all six P100 starts tables still existed at the last post-close sample, even though every native worker had exited. A full collection freed the three unheld waves, **not** the three whose public readers were still held. With scoped retirement, no probed descriptor, starts array or encoder survived *any* post-close sample, without forcing a collection. Holding the same nine expired readers cost ~369 MiB less current RSS in each comparison; mean six-wave wall was 1.009× ordinary. This identifies a specific object-lifetime problem and fix on CPU, **not** the cause of S17's GPU-process high-water series.

**What the references show.** Each cell started near 852 MiB, verified all 16,119,767 document starts anew in each wave, and left the default cyclic collector enabled (thresholds `700,10,10`; one automatic gen-0 collection in each primary cell). Waves 1–3 retained no public handle. `ArchiveRecords` and the bound methods in its `SpanValues` form a cycle; ordinary weakrefs counted 1, 2, 3 sets of objects even without handles. Waves 4–6 kept only the three `Reader`s from each closed wave. After the first full GC, ordinary weakrefs identified *exactly* those three descriptors, their arrays and encoders. Dropping the handles and collecting again left zero. Both retire processes had zero throughout and refused all nine stale callbacks each before dispatch. There were zero registered file descriptors held at every post-close sample, including in ordinary. Closing FDs, reaping children, breaking cycles and revoking exported callbacks are distinct obligations.

**What a reader actually did.** Each fresh whole-P100 `BranchGrant` backed one m20 process and one positional process. The two registered 32-byte B2 literals produced two and one verified spans at their historical positions. A third common key was certified over the raw-eight cap, not truncated or treated as absent. Per wave: five `R` rows/frames (15,461 bytes incl. handshake); two native positional keys in one frame, 128 ranks and **86,488 LF steps**; three contents and three following preads, 144 bytes, 48 FP32 value components. Independent document-bounded reads and an independent mean calculation matched the emitted bindings and values; complete evidence and row traffic hashes matched across all 24 waves. Native locate median was 1.664 s per positive pair (range 1.569–2.160 s). These were issued capability checks, not positions chosen by a learned program.

**Price and scope.** Held-GC RSS savings were 386,953,216 and 387,022,848 bytes vs the required 257,916,272 (two tables). This is a process difference, not a claim that every byte belonged to an ndarray. Mean total open/check, query and close over six waves: ordinary 5.335 / 11.765 / .479 s; retire 5.818 / 11.443 / .464 s. Mean primary wall **17.586 → 17.738 s**, no faster-service claim; interpreter-inclusive 26.249 → 26.378 s. The two diagnostic GCs came *after* primary timing. All 24 m20 and 24 positional workers exited cleanly; max individual native peaks 109.2 and 558.0 MiB, not additive process RSS. [`ScopedRecords`](scoped_records.py) verifies the exact owner/grant/descriptor, checks both transports, and never lends its raw backing callbacks. Use this boundary for one synchronous owner; it does not revoke externally captured raw owners, returned tensors, or values held for backpropagation. It isn't a threaded locator, protection against hostile Python, or an indefinite-service footprint bound.
