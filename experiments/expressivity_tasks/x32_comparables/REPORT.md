# Research: Comparables for "the tape machine" — a slot-KV memory with five fixed architectural operations

**Scope note (what we are comparing against).** The object under study is a slot-based (key, value, strength, birth-time) store inside a neural layer with five *non-learned* operations — APPEND / CORRECT / POP / DECAY / MERGE — content-addressed softmax reads, a tunable slot budget, a per-window "pressure register", and a certified result: 736 slots learn a^k b^k to 99% in-distribution and hold 0.98 at 2×/4× OOD depth where matched gated-delta-rule and tanh-recurrence controls fall to 0.50, with the OOD boundary at the budget (736 < ~1152 needed at 9×; 2944 slots hold 0.947 at 9×).

**Validation limitation, disclosed up front.** The DeltaStack paper body (PMLR v306 PDF, OpenReview PDF) could not be retrieved in this run — every fetch of the PDF aborted or returned a JS shell. Where I make claims about DeltaStack I therefore rest on three things I *did* retrieve and read: (i) the PMLR abstract/BibTeX page, (ii) the authors' official GitHub README, and (iii) the authors' official reference implementation source (`fla-latest/fla/layers/delta_stack.py`), which is stronger evidence than prose for op-level questions. `source_check` was attempted on the DeltaStack claim and returned status **unclear** with "automated semantic support or contradiction assessment is unavailable", so I fell back to reading the original sources directly. Items I could not verify are listed in **Missing evidence** and are not presented as findings.

## Summary

The literature splits cleanly on two axes that our design deliberately crosses: **state growth** (fixed / bounded / content-driven) and **who manages the state** (learned controller / fixed architecture). Every system that grows or re-addresses memory *and* was published before 2026 puts a learned network in charge of the management decisions (NTM, DNC, NRAM, Stack-RNN, NPI, Raven, DeltaStack); every system with zero learned management keeps a *fixed* state and therefore cannot do structural state tracking (DeltaNet, Mamba, linear attention, TTT, Titans, Lattice, MemoryLLM). The nearest published cousin is **DeltaStack** (Nguyen et al., ICML 2026), which shares our efficiency story (stack ops as rank-1 delta-rule updates, fully parallel over sequence length) and our diagnosis (fixed state → hierarchy ceiling) but differs on the two axes that carry our claim: its push/pop decisions are a **learned softmax head** over a **learned pointer displacement**, its stack is **fixed power-of-2 depth** with pointer *clamping* at the boundary (no merge, no pressure signal), and its "pop" moves a pointer without destroying anything. The literature already contains the *phenomenon* we certify — Delétang et al. (ICLR 2023) show that only networks with "a stack or memory tape" generalize on non-regular tasks, and Merrill et al. (ICML 2024) prove fixed-state SSMs cannot state-track — so our novelty is **not** the direction of the result but the **measured, per-instance OOD boundary tied to a physical slot budget**, plus the zero-learned-management design point and the pressure signal.

## Findings

### 1. The ancestors: NTM and DNC put a learned controller in charge, and that is what killed them

1. **Claim:** Neural Turing Machines (Graves, Wayne, Danihelka, arXiv:1410.5401, Oct 2014) introduced a fixed-size N×M memory matrix read and written by *differentiable soft heads* whose addressing is learned — content-based addressing (cosine similarity to a key, softmaxed) interpolated with location-based addressing (a shift + sharpening). Everything about management — what to write, where, and when — is produced by the controller and trained by gradient descent. **Sources:** [Neural Turing Machines](https://arxiv.org/abs/1410.5401). **Support:** direct evidence (abstract + the standard description of the addressing pipeline corroborated by the follow-on literature below). **Confidence:** high.

2. **Claim:** The Differentiable Neural Computer (Graves et al., *Nature* 538, 2016; arXiv:1603.04467) added two mechanisms on top of NTM: **dynamic memory allocation** via a usage vector (so slots are freed and reused rather than overwritten by address), and **temporal links** recorded in an N×N link matrix so the controller can traverse write order. Management is still entirely learned. **Sources:** [DNC (Nature)](https://www.nature.com/articles/nature20101); [DeepMind DNC announcement](https://deepmind.google/blog/differentiable-neural-computers/); internals as described in [Improved Addressing in the DNC (Csordás, NIPS 2018 workshop)](https://people.idsia.ch/~csordas/nips2018.pdf) and [Robust and Scalable DNC for QA (ACL W18-2606)](https://aclanthology.org/W18-2606.pdf). **Support:** direct evidence for existence/design; **interpretation** for the "this is what killed them" causal claim. **Confidence:** high on design, medium on causality.

3. **Claim:** The failure modes reported for the NTM/DNC line are precisely *learned-management* failure modes: (a) content addresses that are noisy or nearly flat, causing **aliasing** between slots; (b) noise accumulating in the temporal links so chained reads degrade; (c) training runs that converge to a solution which **ignores the external memory** and just uses the controller, because early memory reads are noisy; (d) dense N×N bookkeeping that is O(N²) in space and per-step work, capping practical N. **Sources:** [Csordás (2018)](https://people.idsia.ch/~csordas/nips2018.pdf) for (a)(b); [ACL W18-2606](https://aclanthology.org/W18-2606.pdf) for (c); [Huynh, U. Chicago thesis](https://knowledge.uchicago.edu/nanna/record/3022/files/Huynh_uchicago_0330D_15781.pdf) for (d). **Support:** interpretation (secondary analyses of the primary systems). **Confidence:** medium-high.

4. **Claim (researcher inference):** The structural reason the NTM/DNC line lost to Transformers is not that learned addressing is unlearnable but that it is **sequential and per-token**: every token requires a read, a write, and (for DNC) usage/link updates, so the model cannot be trained with a chunk-parallel kernel. Attention replaced the tape with the KV cache — content-addressed softmax reads with *no* write management at all — which is exactly the trade our design rejects in the other direction. This is an inference from the two designs' structure, not a claim any source makes. **Support:** researcher inference. **Confidence:** medium.

### 2. The differentiable-abstract-machine lineage: architectural ops existed, but almost nobody produced OOD receipts with matched controls

5. **Claim:** Stack-RNN (Joulin & Mikolov, NeurIPS 2015, arXiv:1503.01007) attaches a trainable continuous-valued stack to an RNN; push/pop actions are learned from gradients (with optional supervision on stack behavior), and it learns algorithmic sequence transformations (e.g. bigram flipping, temporal-order classification) that plain RNNs fail at. **Sources:** [NeurIPS 2015 paper page](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2015/hash/26657d5ff9020d2abefe558796b99584-Abstract.html). **Support:** direct evidence (abstract). **Confidence:** high on design, medium on the exact generalization claim — **the PDF would not extract in this run**, so I cannot quote their train-length/test-length protocol.

6. **Claim:** The "unbounded differentiable memory" family (Grefenstette et al., *Learning to Transduce with Unbounded Memories*, NeurIPS 2015) implemented stack / queue / deque controllers with a differentiable "stutter" skip so the memory can grow with the input. This is the true ancestor of "growth with architectural ops", and it is *not* stack-only — deques already cover our APPEND/POP semantics. **Sources:** cited here as must-read prior art; **I did not retrieve it in this run** (no URL asserted). **Support:** interpretation from the citing literature (e.g. [Nondeterministic Stack RNNs, ACL 2020](https://aclanthology.org/2020.conll-1.41.pdf) groups "Joulin and Mikolov 2015; Grefenstette et al." as the differentiable-stack line). **Confidence:** medium.

7. **Claim:** Neural GPU (Kaiser & Sutskever, arXiv:1511.08228) — *not* Kurach; the task brief conflates the two — abandons explicit memory ops for a convolutional GRU over a 2-D grid, and demonstrates length generalization on binary addition/multiplication. **Sources:** [Neural GPUs Learn Algorithms](https://arxiv.org/html/1511.08228v3). **Support:** direct evidence. **Confidence:** high.

8. **Claim:** The Neural GPU's OOD claim is the canonical example of an OOD claim that is *distribution-conditional*: Price, Zaremba & Sutskever (arXiv:1611.00736) show that a Neural GPU with near-perfect generalization on decimal multiplication up to 100 digits still produces **wrong answers on highly-symmetric, atypical inputs**, and that performance improves mainly through curriculum design and larger models. **Sources:** [Extensions and Limitations of the Neural GPU](https://arxiv.org/html/1611.00736v2); [OpenAI summary](https://openai.com/index/extensions-and-limitations-of-the-neural-gpu/); [Improving the Neural GPU Architecture for Algorithm Learning](https://arxiv.org/html/1702.08727v2). **Support:** direct evidence. **Confidence:** high.

9. **Claim:** Neural Random-Access Machines (Kurach, Andrychowicz, Sutskever, ICLR 2016, arXiv:1511.06392) manipulate and dereference **pointers** into a variable-size external RAM, trained from input–output examples only, on tasks requiring pointer chasing (linked lists, trees, sorting). Management is learned; the "growth" is architectural (variable-size memory) but the *use* of it is learned. **Sources:** [NRAM](https://arxiv.org/abs/1511.06392); [Google Research page](https://research.google/pubs/neural-random-access-machines/). **Support:** direct evidence. **Confidence:** high.

10. **Claim:** Neural Programmer-Interpreters (Reed & de Freitas, ICLR 2016, arXiv:1511.06279) compose a task-agnostic recurrent core with a **persistent key-value program memory** (memory of *programs*, not data), plus a scratchpad; it learns from fully-supervised execution traces. The well-known sort-generalization result is train-on-lengths-up-to-20, test-beyond — an early structural generalization anecdote, but with weak baselines and heavy trace supervision. **Sources:** [NPI](https://arxiv.org/abs/1511.06279); [lecture slides stating the ≤20 train / >20 test protocol](https://www.cs.toronto.edu/~florian/courses/imitation_learning/lectures/Lecture5.pdf). **Support:** direct evidence for design; interpretation for "weak baselines". **Confidence:** medium-high.

11. **Claim:** Differentiable Forth (Bošnjak, Rocktäschel, Naradowsky, Riedel, ICML 2017, arXiv:1605.06640) is the cleanest **hybrid** data point: the *program skeleton and control flow* are fixed architectural prior knowledge, while the *local actions/word semantics* are learned and the interpreter is differentiable end-to-end; it solves sequence sorting and addition connected to an LSTM. **Sources:** [PMLR v70](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v70/bosnjak17a.html). **Support:** direct evidence. **Confidence:** high. **Relevance:** this is the precedent for "fix the management, learn the content" — but Forth fixes *program structure*, not *memory-resource management*, and it never claims depth-OOD.

12. **Claim:** The strongest formal-language receipts in the stack line come from DuSell & Chiang: a differentiable **nondeterministic** stack whose configuration set grows with input length (CoNLL 2020, arXiv:2010.04674) and the follow-up theory paper (ICLR 2023, arXiv:2210.01343) showing such stacks give strictly more recognition power than fixed-cell RNNs, with memory-limited linear-time variants. Separately, Suzgun et al. (arXiv:1911.03329) show memory-augmented RNNs with a differentiable stack learn **generalized Dyck** languages. **Sources:** [NS-RNN ACL](https://aclanthology.org/2020.conll-1.41/); [arXiv 2210.01343](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2210.01343); [Suzgun et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1911.03329). **Support:** direct evidence. **Confidence:** high. **Relevance:** these are the systems that *do* have structural-generalization receipts on Dyck-family tasks — and they are all stack-discipline (LIFO, pointer at top) with learned push/pop weights.

13. **Claim:** The single most important prior-art item for our headline claim is Delétang et al., *Neural Networks and the Chomsky Hierarchy* (ICLR 2023, arXiv:2207.02098): across 20,910 models and 15 tasks, RNNs and Transformers fail to generalize on non-regular tasks, LSTMs solve some regular tasks, and — quoting the abstract — "only networks augmented with structured memory (such as a stack or **memory tape**) can successfully generalize on non-regular tasks". **Sources:** [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.02098), [ICLR poster](https://iclr.cc/virtual/2023/poster/12189), [DeepMind code](https://github.com/google-deepmind/neural_networks_chomsky_hierarchy). **Support:** direct evidence (abstract, verbatim). **Confidence:** high. **Consequence:** the *direction* of our result is already in the literature, and the phrase "memory tape" is already taken.

14. **Claim:** The theoretical half of our separation result is also already published: Merrill, Petty & Sabharwal, *The Illusion of State in State-Space Models* (ICML 2024, arXiv:2404.08819) prove that S4/Mamba-class SSMs (and by the same argument fixed-state linear-attention/delta-rule recurrences) sit in TC⁰ and therefore cannot solve state-tracking problems such as permutation composition / S5, and show that "only a minimal change allows SSMs to express and learn state tracking". **Sources:** [PMLR v235](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v235/merrill24a.html), [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.08819). **Support:** direct evidence. **Confidence:** high. **Consequence:** we must not present "fixed-state models provably can't do this" as a contribution; only the *empirical boundary and its budget scaling* is ours.

15. **Claim:** I could not identify a paper matching "Differentiable Pushdown (Du et al.)". The nearest real items are the Grefenstette stack/queue/deque line and DuSell & Chiang's stack RNNs. **Support:** missing evidence — see below.

### 3. The modern slot-memory lineage: capacity, not structure — and growth is either a cache or a training-time artifact

16. **Claim:** Product-key memories (Lample et al., NeurIPS 2019, arXiv:1907.05242) make memory "very large by design … up to a billion parameters with a negligible computational overhead", and a 12-layer PKM model beats a 24-layer Transformer while being twice as fast at inference. The memory is **fixed at design time, sparse-key addressed, and trained**; there is no write/overwrite/delete semantics and no structural claim. **Sources:** [NeurIPS 2019](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2019/hash/9d8df73a3cfbf3c5b47bc9b50f214aff-Paper.pdf), [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.05242). **Support:** direct evidence (abstract, verbatim numbers). **Confidence:** high.

17. **Claim:** Memory Layers at Scale (Berges et al., Meta FAIR, arXiv:2412.09764, Dec 2024; ICML 2025 PMLR v267) takes trainable key-value memory layers "beyond proof-of-concept" using trainable product-quantized keys, reporting downstream gains at contemporary scale, and releases an open-source GPU-efficient sparse product-key implementation. The claim is **capacity per FLOP**, never structural generalization. **Sources:** [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.09764), [PMLR v267](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v267/berges25a.html), [Meta AI](https://ai.meta.com/research/publications/memory-layers-at-scale/), [code](http://github.com/facebookresearch/memory). **Support:** direct evidence for the claim shape; **confidence medium** on exact scale numbers (I did not re-verify the per-configuration parameter counts).

18. **Claim:** Memorizing Transformers (Wu et al., ICLR 2022, arXiv:2203.08913) use approximate kNN lookup into a **non-differentiable** memory of past (key, value) pairs; the memory grows with context and — importantly for us — the abstract states that "models trained with a small memory show gains from using a much larger memory at inference time". That is prior art for "the store-size knob transfers at test time". **Sources:** [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.08913), [ICLR PDF](https://openreview.net/pdf?id=TrjbxzRcnf-). **Support:** direct evidence (abstract quote from the arXiv PDF text). **Confidence:** high. **Relevance:** our budget knob is *not* novel as a knob; what differs is that ours is certified as a *failure boundary*, not just a benefit.

19. **Claim:** Lattice (Karami, Pascanu, Mirrokni — Google Research / DeepMind, arXiv:2504.05646, Apr 2025) compresses the KV cache into a **fixed number of memory slots** by formulating compression as online optimization and deriving a dynamic update rule from a single gradient step; each slot is updated only with information **orthogonal to its current state** ("only novel, non-redundant" content), with state- and input-dependent gating. **Sources:** [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.05646). **Support:** direct evidence (abstract/search-extracted text). **Confidence:** high on mechanism. **Relevance:** this is the closest published relative of our MERGE (budget-forced compression of novel content onto an existing slot) — and it is derived as a *learned/optimization* rule rather than a fixed architectural degradation.

20. **Claim:** Two 2026 slot models sit closer to our "slots + decay + selective update" shape than anything from 2019–2022: **Raven** (arXiv:2607.25357) keeps a fixed set of memory slots and uses an **input-dependent learned router** to decay and update only a selected subset, reporting extrapolation to context lengths up to **16×** training length, explicitly positioned between "SSMs update too diffusely" and "SWA updates too rigidly"; **RAM-Net** (arXiv:2602.11958) keeps a fixed-size array of independent slots with a sparse address decoder, reporting access to **8× fewer state elements per step than Mamba2**. **Sources:** [Raven](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.25357), [RAM-Net](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.11958). **Support:** direct evidence (abstracts); read mechanism and OOD-baseline details **not** determinable from abstracts. **Confidence:** medium.

21. **Claim (researcher inference):** Across this whole lineage the growth axis is real but *uninteresting for our claim*: memory either grows as a passive cache of inputs (Memorizing Transformers, kNN-LM) or is fixed and trained (PKM, Memory Layers, Lattice, Raven, RAM-Net). **None** of them triggers growth from *content* and then manages it with fixed operations. **Support:** researcher inference from the above sources. **Confidence:** medium-high (absence-of-evidence argument).

### 4. The test-time-memory lineage: a fixed-size state that is *updated* well, not a state that grows

22. **Claim:** Titans (Behrouz, Zhong, Mirrokni, NeurIPS 2025, arXiv:2501.00663) makes the long-term memory a **neural module that memorizes at test time** — its state is a parameterized memory updated during inference (surprise/novelty-driven), combined with attention in three architectural variants (memory-as-context, memory-as-gate, memory-as-layer). The state is **fixed-size**; the contribution is *what* is written and *how* it decays, not how the store grows. **Sources:** [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.00663), [NeurIPS 2025 PDF](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/file/a4ca07aa108036f80cbb5b82285fd4b1-Paper-Conference.pdf). **Support:** direct evidence (abstract). **Confidence:** high.

23. **Claim:** Test-Time Training (Sun et al., ICML 2025 PMLR v267, arXiv:2407.04620) makes the hidden state *itself a model* and the update rule *a step of self-supervised learning*, yielding linear-complexity layers with more expressive **fixed-size** states (TTT-Linear, TTT-MLP). Scales demonstrated in the 100M–1B+ range with long-context wins. **Sources:** [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.04620), [PMLR v267](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v267/sun25h.html). **Support:** direct evidence. **Confidence:** high.

24. **Claim:** I could not identify a paper by "Sander et al." titled or abbreviated **MOVES** on self-updatable memory. The closest real items I found are **MemoryLLM** (Wang et al., arXiv:2402.04624) — a *fixed-size* latent memory pool inside a Llama-2-7B backbone whose contents are rewritten as the model reads, with controlled exponential forgetting (secondary description of the pool: ~32 layers × 7,680 memory tokens × 4,096 dims) — and **MoVE: Mixture of Value Embeddings** (arXiv:2601.22887), which scales *parametric* memory, not slots. **Sources:** [MemoryLLM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.04624), [secondary description of the pool](https://theaiarchs.com/n/arxiv2402.04624), [MoVE](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.22887). **Support:** direct for MemoryLLM's existence/claim; **missing evidence** for the intended "MOVES". **Confidence:** low on the identification, medium on the substitutes.

25. **Claim:** What every member of this lineage shares is the *state abstraction*: memory = a fixed-size, densely-written parameter block, update = a learned (often gradient-based) rule, read = attention or a linear map. **Nothing in the lineage grows.** Therefore the correct comparison for us is not "Titans/TTT are worse" but "Titans/TTT optimize the write rule inside a fixed state, which is a *different* axis from buying capacity". **Sources:** as above. **Support:** interpretation. **Confidence:** high.

### 5. The closest published cousin — DeltaStack, analyzed against our design

**What it is.** Nguyen, Momeni, Chaubey, Xiao, Liu, *Push, Pop, Parallelize: Stack-Augmented Linear Attention via the Delta Rule*, ICML 2026, PMLR 306:92579–92592, poster 8 Jul 2026. Abstract claims: fixed-size DeltaNet state "fundamentally struggle[s] to capture the recursive, hierarchical structures intrinsic to natural languages"; DeltaStack adds "a lightweight, differentiable stack"; stack ops are cast as **linear delta-rule updates** so the model is "fully parallelizable over sequence length"; it proves DeltaStack extends DeltaNet's expressivity to regular **and** hierarchical languages; it beats DeltaNet and Stack-Attention on formal-language benchmarks and beats DeltaNet baselines on perplexity/zero-shot "across scales up to 760M parameters". Sources: [PMLR](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v306/nguyen26i.html), [ICML poster](https://icml.cc/virtual/2026/poster/61979), [code](https://github.com/teeann/DeltaStack).

26. **Claim (op-level, read from the reference implementation):** the stack is **not** a slot array with keys/values/strengths. The layer computes `action_proj = nn.Linear(hidden_size, num_heads*3)`, takes a **softmax over three action logits**, sets `delta_ptr = push_prob - pop_prob`, integrates it with a **cumsum** to get a continuous head position, and **clamps** it to `[0, stack_size-1]`. Stack reads/writes are then realized by *interleaving* the k/v/beta streams (doubling sequence length) and running `chunk_delta_rule` over them, i.e. **push and pop are rank-1 delta-rule updates to a linear-attention state indexed by the clamped pointer**. **Sources:** [delta_stack.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/teeann/DeltaStack/main/fla-latest/fla/layers/delta_stack.py) (`_generate_soft_pointer`, `fused_interleave`, `chunk_delta_rule` calls; `stack_kappa = nn.Parameter(tensor([2.0]))` for pointer sharpening). **Support:** direct evidence. **Confidence:** high.

27. **Claim:** Consequently the axis-by-axis comparison is:

| Axis | DeltaStack | Tape machine |
|---|---|---|
| State size | **Fixed, bounded** `stack_size`, must be a **power of 2** (the fused Triton kernel indexes with `tl.arange(0, S)`; a warning fires otherwise). Default 8 in the layer; 64 in the shipped `lab.json`; **resettable at runtime** via `set_stack_size()` / `val_stack_size` (README: "Eval-time stack depth (default 256), for length generalization beyond the trained depth"). | **Budgeted**, content-driven occupancy; the budget is a knob and an explicit degradation policy exists at saturation. |
| Management authority | **Learned**: a linear head → softmax → soft (continuous) pointer displacement. Plus a learned `stack_kappa` sharpening scalar. | **Architectural, zero learned management**: the five ops are fixed programs; only content projections are learned. |
| Op set | {push, pop, no-op} as *soft pointer motion*. **No delete** — a pop only moves the head; content stays in the delta-rule state until overwritten. No strength, no birth-time, no decay, no merge. | APPEND / CORRECT / POP (read-then-destroy) / DECAY (scheduled death) / MERGE (budget-forced delta-rule compression onto the newest slot). |
| Behavior at capacity | Pointer **clamps** at the boundary; further pushes pile onto the top position → silent, unreported lossy overwrite. | APPEND **degrades to MERGE**, and the degradation is **counted** and exposed as a per-token pressure signal. |
| Reads | Linear-attention (delta-rule) read of the stack state at the pointer. | Content-addressed **softmax** mixture over slots — genuinely attention-shaped, GEMM-able. |
| Parallelism | **Yes** — chunkwise-parallel over sequence length (this is the paper's headline). | **Yes** — attention-shaped GEMM reads; maintenance is O(1) amortized via birth-timestamps, lazy log-space decay, free lists. |
| OOD receipts | Formal-language suite (Dyck-k, stack manipulation, reverse string, mod-arith ± brackets, parity, even-pairs, cycle-nav, duplicate-string) + LM length extrapolation (train 4k → eval 16k) reported by third-party summary. **A `val_stack_size` knob proves they evaluate beyond trained depth**; the outcome table is unverified here. | Certification: 99% ID, 0.98 at 2×/4× OOD; fixed-state controls collapse to 0.50; boundary at the budget (736 < ~1152 at 9×; 2944 → 0.947 at 9×). |

**Sources:** DeltaStack README + `delta_stack.py` + `experiment_configs/lab.json` + `data_dir/fl_tasks/*` (retrieved via the GitHub tree API: `dyck.py`, `stack_manipulation.py`, `reverse_string.py`, `mod_arith_no_brack.py`, `mod_arith_w_brack.py`, `parity.py`, `even_pairs.py`, `cycle_nav.py`, `duplicate_string.py`); abstract-level results from PMLR; the "~14% average gain over the best linear baselines", "Dyck nearly matching sequential Stack-RNN", "760M: perplexity better, zero-shot 52.0% vs 51.4%", "~9% memory overhead, ~19k tokens/s at 16k context", and the "modular arithmetic with brackets still hard to learn from scratch" limitation come from a **third-party AI summary** ([Lacuna](https://lacuna.tiptreesystems.com/work/push-pop-parallelize-stack-augmented-linear-attention)) — **label these as second-hand and unverified**; do not cite them as the paper's numbers. **Support:** direct evidence for code-level facts, interpretation/second-hand for performance numbers. **Confidence:** high on axes, low on DeltaStack's numeric results.

28. **Claim (the sharpest structural point in this report):** DeltaStack's own framing undercuts the "growth" framing of our contribution. It argues that **fixed-size** states fail on hierarchy and then fixes the problem with a **still fixed-size** (bounded, power-of-2) stack whose only new property is *re-addressability plus structural ops*. Its abstract says the stack extends expressivity to model hierarchical languages while the state remains bounded. Therefore "our state grows" cannot be the load-bearing differentiator; the load-bearing differentiators must be **(i) who manages (nobody learns anything), (ii) the saturation policy (merge + telemetry vs clamp), and (iii) the budget-tied OOD boundary**, which DeltaStack does not report at all. **Sources:** DeltaStack abstract + code (bounded power-of-2 stack), combined by inference. **Support:** interpretation built on direct evidence. **Confidence:** high as an inference; it is falsifiable by the DeltaStack PDF, which I could not read.

### 6. The fast-weight lineage as the fixed-state family our separation argues against

29. **Claim:** DeltaNet (Yang, Wang, Zhou, Zhang; NeurIPS 2024, arXiv:2406.06484) replaces the additive update of linear attention with the **delta rule**, giving a fixed-size matrix state with provably better in-context retrieval and a chunkwise-parallel training algorithm. Its lineage is explicitly "Linear Transformers Are Secretly Fast Weight Programmers" (Schlag, Irie, Schmidhuber, arXiv:2102.11174) and Schmidhuber's 1992 fast weights. **Sources:** [DeltaNet arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.06484), [NeurIPS 2024 poster](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2024/poster/93040); the fast-weight attribution is stated in the DeltaStack layer docstring, which credits both. **Support:** direct evidence. **Confidence:** high.

30. **Claim:** Linear attention (Katharopoulos et al., 2020, arXiv:2006.16236) and Mamba (Gu & Dao, arXiv:2312.00752) are the two canonical fixed-state compressors; both are O(1) state per layer and both are the object of the Merrill et al. impossibility results for state tracking. **Sources:** [linear attention](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.16236), [Mamba](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.00752), [Illusion of State](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.08819). **Support:** direct evidence. **Confidence:** high.

31. **Claim:** The correct role of this family in our paper is **control**, not competitor: it is the family for which "0.50 at 2×/4×" is *predicted* by theory, so the matched gated-delta-rule and tanh-recurrence runs are a **sanity check on the task and the training budget**, not a surprise. A reviewer will say exactly this. **Support:** researcher inference. **Confidence:** high.

### 7. Live adjacent prior art that was not in the original brief but must be handled

## (b) Closest cousins — deep dive

### 1. DeltaStack (ICML 2026) — nearest by mechanism and by diagnosis; different on the axis that carries our claim

- **Shared:** diagnosis (fixed state = hierarchy ceiling), efficiency route (ops as rank-1 delta-rule updates; chunk-parallel training; attention-shaped GEMMs), and the "budget is a knob" instinct (`set_stack_size`, `val_stack_size=256` for beyond-trained-depth evaluation).
- **Different (decisive):** management authority. Theirs is `Linear → softmax(3) → push_prob − pop_prob → cumsum → clamp` — a **learned** controller with a **soft, continuous** head pointer. Ours is five fixed programs and zero learned management.
- **Different (second order):** saturation policy. They **clamp**; we **merge and report**. Their boundary behavior is silent; ours emits a per-token pressure signal, which is the only mechanism I found in any system in this survey that makes capacity exhaustion *observable to the model at inference time*.
- **Different (third order):** POP semantics. Theirs is head motion; content persists in the delta-rule state. Ours destroys the slot (free-list reuse). This matters because read-then-destroy is what makes our O(1) maintenance argument airtight rather than relying on overwrite.
- **Where they are ahead of us:** (i) they prove expressivity results (single-layer Dyck recognition); (ii) they have LM-scale evidence (340M/760M, 15B tokens, PPL + zero-shot) — we have none; (iii) they have a hardware story with real Triton kernels and throughput numbers; (iv) they compare against a *broad* baseline set (`deltanet`, `gateddeltanet`, `deltaproduct`, `rwkv6`, `transformer_causal`, `stack_rnn`, `lstm` are all selectable in their harness) — this is nearly the matched-control discipline we claim as distinctive, so our "matched fixed-state controls" framing is **less unique than it looks**.
- **Where we are ahead:** zero-learned-management design point; a *quantified* capacity boundary (they have a knob, not a boundary); pressure telemetry; destructive POP; strength/birth-time with exact scheduled death; and the explicit claim that the OOD cliff coincides with a physical resource limit.
- **Unverified:** their OOD numbers. Until I read the PDF, treat "DeltaStack has no budget-boundary analysis" as *our reading of the abstract + README*, not a fact about the paper.

### 2. DNC (2016) — nearest by ambition, furthest by mechanism

- **Shared:** a store with allocation/deallocation semantics (usage vector ≈ our free-list), ordered access (temporal links ≈ our birth-time ordering), and multiple read modes.
- **Different:** every management decision is a learned, differentiable vector; the store is fixed-capacity at design; bookkeeping is O(N²) (links); training is sequential per token.
- **Why this matters for us:** DNC is the historical proof that *learned* allocation + ordering is learnable but fragile (aliasing, link noise, memory-ignoring optima). Our design is, in effect, the DNC hypothesis run the other way: **fix the management, learn only content** — and the pressure register is a direct architectural answer to DNC's "converges to ignoring memory" failure, because degradation becomes an observable output rather than a hidden optimizer choice. This is a good framing sentence for the paper, and it is an *interpretation* (mine), not a claim from any DNC paper.

### 3. Titans (2025) / the test-time-memory lineage — nearest by "memory that lives inside the layer"

- **Shared:** memory as a first-class in-layer object updated at inference; a notion of importance/decay; attention-shaped interaction with the backbone.
- **Different:** Titans' memory is a **fixed-size parameter block** updated by a learned, gradient-flavored rule; there are no discrete slots, no addresses, no death, no budget, and crucially **no capacity** — adding capacity means adding parameters. Our tape's capacity is a *runtime integer* with a documented degradation curve.
- **The honest comparison:** Titans/TTT answer "how should a fixed state be written?"; we answer "what if the state is a store with a budget?" These are complementary, and a reviewer will ask why we did not simply attach our store to a Titan-style memory module. That is a good future-work line, not a weakness.

### Late additions to the cousin set (found during this survey, closer than expected on specific mechanisms)

- **EDA (2026)** ≈ our CORRECT's *address decoupling* — must cite, must scope.
- **Raven (2026)** ≈ our DECAY + selective update, with a learned router instead of fixed ops; claims 16× length extrapolation. Closest on the "slots + decay + routing" shape.
- **Lattice (2025)** ≈ our MERGE (novel-content-only compression into a fixed slot count).

## (c) Honest novelty assessment

**Genuinely new (as far as this survey reaches):**

1. **Zero-learned-management growth with graceful budget pressure.** Every growing/re-addressable system I found either learns the control policy (NTM, DNC, NRAM, Stack-RNN, NPI, Raven, DeltaStack) or does not grow (PKM, Memory Layers, Lattice, TTT, Titans, MemoryLLM, DeltaNet, Mamba). "Content triggers growth; a fixed program manages it; saturation is a merge, not a crash or an eviction" is not, to my reading, an existing published design point. *Confidence: medium-high; this is an absence-of-evidence claim, and the search was not exhaustive.*
2. **The pressure register as a first-class per-token output.** I found no system that exposes "fraction of appends that degraded this window" as a model-visible signal. Nearest analogues are offline diagnostics (cache hit rate, eviction counts) and Titans' surprise gate (an *input* to the write rule, not an *output*). *Confidence: medium.*
3. **A certified OOD boundary that traces to a physical slot budget, with matched fixed-state controls.** Delétang et al. established the qualitative phenomenon; Merrill et al. established the theory; **nobody I found reports the per-instance transition point and shows it moves with the budget** (736 slots ⟹ collapse at 9×; 2944 slots ⟹ 0.947 at 9×). If that scaling law holds across budgets and seeds, it is the paper's most citable result. *Confidence: medium-high on novelty of form; the direction of the effect is prior art.*
4. **Recency-addressed, error-correcting writes** — *partially* new. The *error-correcting overwrite* is DeltaNet's delta rule (2024) and fast-weight programming (1992/2021); the *address decoupling* is EDA (2026). What is ours is that the correction address is **the slot written at the previous token** — a free, temporal address requiring no extra parameters or learned projection, and one that is well-defined precisely because we have discrete slots with birth-times. Claim it as a *narrow mechanism*, not as a concept. *Confidence: high on the recombination analysis.*

**Recombination (say so explicitly in the paper):**

- Content-addressed softmax reads = attention (Bahdanau/Graves; Vaswani et al.).
- Pop/consume semantics = stack/deque controllers (Grefenstette 2015) and DNC deallocation.
- Recency/LRU aging + exponential forgetting = cache-replacement and gated-linear-attention folklore; MemoryLLM's controlled exponential forgetting; Titans' decay.
- Merge-under-pressure onto an existing slot = delta-rule write to a bounded store (DeltaNet, DeltaStack's clamped top slot) crossed with Lattice's "novel, orthogonal content only" compression.
- O(1) amortized maintenance via birth-timestamps / lazy decay / free lists = standard allocator engineering plus the lazy-decay trick that decay-based linear attention already uses.
- a^k b^k as a probe = Delétang's Chomsky-hierarchy suite; counter-language and state-tracking benchmarks.
- "Store-size is a test-time knob" = Memorizing Transformers (2022) already reported gains from inference-time memory enlargement.

**Recombination that is nonetheless load-bearing:** the *combination* — fixed ops + budget + telemetry + destructive pop + softmax reads — is what makes the certified boundary possible, and that combination is not in any single prior system.

## Contradictions

1. **Delétang et al. vs. our efficiency story.** Their abstract reports that structured-memory ("stack or memory tape") networks *can* generalize on non-regular tasks, but the paper is widely read as also finding these models need far more data and training time than the failing baselines. That sits in tension with a headline like "learns a^k b^k to 99% in-distribution" being *cheap*. [arXiv:2207.02098](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.02098). *Recorded, not resolved* — I could not verify the sample-efficiency numbers for our specific task class.
2. **Price et al. vs. Neural GPU's own claim.** "Generalizes to inputs of arbitrary length" versus demonstrable failure on highly-symmetric atypical inputs. [arXiv:1611.00736](https://arxiv.org/html/1611.00736v2). Implication: our OOD claim must specify the *distribution* of OOD depths (uniform vs adversarial/degenerate), or the same critique applies.
3. **What actually killed DNC.** Two of my sources emphasise different causes — O(N²) bookkeeping ([thesis](https://knowledge.uchicago.edu/nanna/record/3022/files/Huynh_uchicago_0330D_15781.pdf)) versus learned-addressing unreliability/aliasing ([Csordás](https://people.idsia.ch/~csordas/nips2018.pdf)) versus memory-ignoring optima ([ACL W18-2606](https://aclanthology.org/W18-2606.pdf)). All are real; they are not the same cause. *Recorded without resolution.*
4. **DeltaStack's framing contradicts a naive "growth" reading of our contribution.** It argues fixed-size states are the problem, then solves it with a still-bounded stack — meaning bounded + re-addressable + structural ops may be sufficient, and unbounded/content-driven growth may not be the load-bearing property at all. This is simultaneously (a) the strongest support for "the ops matter more than the size" and (b) the strongest challenge to how we currently frame the paper. *Flag for the parent: this is a framing decision, not a fact dispute.*
5. **Our own design is bounded, not growing.** We describe ourselves as having "growth with graceful budget pressure", but 736 and 2944 are both budgets, and saturation degrades to MERGE. On the strict growth axis we are *bounded*, like DeltaStack, not *content-driven-unbounded*, like a stack RNN. The honest axis label is "content-driven **occupancy** at a tunable budget". *Recorded as a self-consistency issue in our own framing.*

## ADDENDUM A — DeltaStack PDF verification (added by the parent session, post-survey)

The survey flagged the unread DeltaStack PDF as the single largest evidence gap. The parent retrieved and read the full paper (PMLR 306:92579–92592; local copy at /tmp/deltastack.pdf, fetched 2026-10-26). Corrections and confirmations to the survey's DeltaStack claims:

**Confirmed from the paper body:**
- Management is **learned**: actions are `softmax(W_α x_t) ∈ Δ³` over {PUSH, POP, NO-OP}; the pointer is a continuous scalar drift `p_t = p_{t−1} + α_push − α_pop` sharpened by a **learnable** Laplacian kernel (γ = softplus(γ̂)). The survey's code-level reading was correct.
- Theory: single-layer DeltaStack recognizes Dyck_{k,D} with embedding dim d = ⌈log₂ k⌉+2 **and stack capacity S ≥ D**; DeltaNet recovered as a special case; NC¹-complete state tracking inherited. Expressivity only — the paper's own Discussion concedes "expressivity does not guarantee learnability."
- LM results: 340M/760M on FineWeb-Edu; 760M zero-shot avg 52.0 vs DeltaNet 51.4; length extrapolation to 16k on CodeParrot/OpenThoughts-Math maintained vs DeltaNet.
- Formal languages (their Table 1, 5-run avg): DeltaStack 79.4 avg vs DeltaNet 65.5; Stack-RNN (sequential reference) 96.3. Per task: Dyck 94.3, Stack Manipulation 96.8, Reverse String 98.8, **Mod Arith w/ Brackets 35.2** (they explicitly struggle; learnability admission in §6).

**Corrections to the survey:**
1. "No capacity analysis" must be softened. They **do** have a stack-size ablation (their Fig. 3: S ∈ {8, 16, 24, 48} on Parity / Reverse String / Stack Manipulation) and their Theorem 4.4 carries an explicit **S ≥ D capacity condition**, with a remark that deeper test-time nesting generalizes by increasing S "without retraining" — a *theoretical* claim from the construction, with no empirical beyond-trained-depth receipt.
2. What remains absent, verified against the full text: any **failure-boundary analysis** — what happens when S < D. Saturation behavior is unexamined in the paper (the pointer clamp lives in the code); there is no degradation policy, no saturation telemetry, and no empirical curve of collapse depth vs budget with matched fixed-state controls. Their capacity story is a monotone-benefit ablation plus a sufficiency theorem, not a boundary measurement.
3. Their baseline breadth is real (DeltaNet, Gated DeltaNet, Gated DeltaProduct, RWKV-7, Transformer, Stack-Attention, LSTM, Stack-RNN at trained lengths) — our "matched controls" discipline is less distinctive than the survey already noted; our distinction is the *boundary characterization*, not the existence of controls.

**Strategic consequences (updated):**
- The quadrant claim (zero-learned-management × content-driven occupancy) survives: DeltaStack sits firmly in the learned-management, bounded-state quadrant.
- DeltaStack's S ≥ D theorem **supports our framing**: capacity binds expressivity — we are the ones who measure where the boundary empirically bites, expose saturation as a signal (pressure), and handle it gracefully (merge) rather than assuming it away or clamping silently.
